\section{Preliminary width and tilt estimates}\label{app:preliminary-estimates}

The estimates here use only the tents at $\pi/8,\pi/4,3\pi/8$.
They apply to a full niche and to any larger partial barrier. In a
partial-turn comparison a horizontal reflection also reflects the
actual barrier; it need not produce another partial-turn objective.

Throughout this appendix put
\[
 r=\sqrt2,\quad k=r-1,\quad c=\cos(\pi/8),\quad s=\sin(\pi/8).
\]
For the diagonal offset, let $\mathcal N_q(x)=(x+q/x)/2$ and set
\[
 b=\mathcal N_2(\mathcal N_2(3/2)),\qquad k_-=2/b-1,\qquad k_+=b-1.
\]
The reciprocal of a square-root upper bound supplies the lower bound.
Useful exact identities and bounds are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preliminary-constants}
 \begin{gathered}
 k^2=1-2k,\quad s/c=k,\quad k+1/k=2r,\quad
 1/s+1/c=4c,\quad 1/s-1/c=4s,\\
 k_-<k<k_+,\qquad \frac25<k<\frac{29}{70},\qquad s>\frac38.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Caps below the unit-width boundary}

The extra-tail height is supplied by the wall geometry. Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 N=1/c=\sqrt{1+k^2},\qquad \eta=1-N+k^2=N(N-1),\qquad t_0=k-\eta,\\
 m_*=(N+\eta/k)/2=(1+k)(1-N/2),\\
 a_*=1-k^2/4,\qquad h_*=k^2/4,\qquad D_*=(k/3)^2/r.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here $\eta$ is the available height of the nearby companion wall at
a diagonal zero, and $m_*$ is exactly the midpoint needed for the
companion support inequality. Choosing $t_0=k-\eta$ makes the omitted-tip
length a diagonal multiple of $\eta$, so its area refund combines directly
with the two triangle payments. The remaining reserves use $k^2$.

\begin{lemma}[Short three-tent bound]\label{lem:preliminary-short}
Let $U$ have height one and projection $[-a,a]$, with $0<a\le1$.
Suppose its roof $A$ is affine on $J=[-a/2,a/2]$, with
$A(-a/2)=1-h$, $A(a/2)=1$, $0\le h<1$.
If a nonnegative barrier $B$ dominates the three actual tents, then
\[
 \int_{[-a,a]\setminus J}A-\int_JB<\underline p<\Mc/2.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Raise the left exterior roof by $h$, put the middle roof at height
one, and retain the right roof. The resulting roof $\widehat A$ is
concave: its left slopes are nonnegative and its right slopes are
nonpositive. It defines a height-one cap $\widehat U$, and its
exterior area exceeds the original by $ah/2$.

The first-quarter supports are unchanged, since the old point
$(a/2,1)$ dominates every raised left point in those directions.
If $\widehat G(t)=h_{\widehat U}(t+\pi/2)$, then
\[
 h_U(t+\pi/2)=
 \max\{\widehat G(t)-h\cos t,\ \cos t-(a/2)\sin t\}.
\]
The wall of the second term is nonpositive on $J$. Thus the
positive two-wall tent of $U$ is obtained from that of $\widehat U$
by lowering its companion wall by $h$.

Put
\[
 u=rh_{\widehat U}(\pi/4)-1,\quad
 v=rh_{\widehat U}(3\pi/4)-1,\quad M_0=(u+v)/2.
\]
The two middle points give $a/2\le u,v\le a$. The lifted cap is
below $A_0(x)=\min(1,1+u-x,1+v+x)$. Write
$D=\int_{[-a,a]\setminus J}(A_0-\widehat A)\ge0$.
The diagonal tent is $(\min(u-k-x,v-h-k+x))_+$.
Suppose the proposed score $P$ is at least $\underline p$.
The rectangle bound $P\le a(1-h/2)$ gives $a\ge\underline p$
and $h\le2(1-\underline p)$. The reference budget in
\cref{eq:reference-half-area-lower} implies $h<a/2$ and $a-h>k$,
so the tent's apex lies in $J$. Exact integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-relaxation}
 \begin{split}
 P\le F_h(a,u,v)-D,\qquad
 F_h={}&a-\frac{(a-u)^2+(a-v)^2}{2}-\frac{ah}{2}\\
 &-(M_0-h/2-k)_+^2\\
 &+\frac{(u-k-a/2)_+^2+(v-h-k-a/2)_+^2}{2}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
In this range, $F_h$ is concave on $[a/2,a]^2$. In the
positive-apex region its Hessian has diagonal entries $-3/2$
plus the corresponding clipping indicator, and off-diagonal
entries $-1/2$. In the other region the clipping terms vanish
and the Hessian is $-\operatorname{Id}$. The first derivatives
agree across the interfaces. Its unclipped critical point is
$u=v=(a+k+h/2)/2$. Thus, with $B_0(a)=a-(a-k)^2/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-diagonal-maximum}
 F_h\le B_0(a)-kh/2-h^2/8,\qquad B_0(1)=2k.
\end{equation}

The boundary comparisons required below are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-geometric-comparisons}
 \begin{aligned}
 B_0(a_*)&<\underline p,\\
 2k-kh_*/2-h_*^2/8&<\underline p,\\
 2k-\tfrac23(t_0-k/2)^2&<\underline p,\\
 1-(1-m_*)^2-(m_*-k)^2&<\underline p,\\
 a_*/2-t_0-h_*/4&>k/3,\qquad 2k-\underline p<D_*.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
They use the same automatically generated enclosures as the reference
comparison. Enclose $k$ by the bounds in
\cref{eq:preliminary-constants}; for $N$, apply two Newton steps from
one to an upper bound for $N^2=2-2k$. Substitute these bounds and the
formula for $\underline p$, reducing squares by $k^2+2k=1$ and
$N^2+2k=2$. This reduces the six comparisons to rational arithmetic.

Since $B_0$ increases up to $1+k$, the first two comparisons force
$a>a_*$ and $h<h_*$. Also $D\le2k-P<D_*$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-stability}
 a>a_*,\qquad h<h_*,\qquad D<D_*.
\end{equation}
Maximizing \cref{eq:short-relaxation} subject to
$u\ge a/2+t_0+h/4$ gives the constrained critical point
\[
 u=a/2+t_0+h/4,\qquad v=a/2+(2k-t_0)/3+h/4.
\]
Its $v$ derivative is zero and its $u$ derivative is $(2k-4t_0)/3<0$.
The value is at most the third line of
\cref{eq:short-geometric-comparisons}. Exchanging the coordinates gives
\[
 u-a/2<t_0+h/4,\qquad v-a/2<t_0+h/4.
\]
The bounds in \cref{eq:short-stability} place the comparison points
in the square, remove both clips, and make the actual apex positive.
With $m=M_0-h/4$, the relaxation is then
\[
 F_h=a-(a-m)^2-(m-k)^2-\frac{(u-v)^2}{4}-kh/2-h^2/8.
\]
If $m\le m_*$, subtract its value from the fourth line of
\cref{eq:short-geometric-comparisons}. The difference is a sum of
squares and the nonnegative terms
$(2m_*-1)(1-a)$ and $2(1+k-2m_*)(m_*-m)$.
This contradicts the high score. Hence $m>m_*$.

We now pay for two genuine extra tents. Define the lifted support deficits
\[
 \delta_L=c+sv-h_{\widehat U}(5\pi/8),\qquad
 \delta_R=c+su-h_{\widehat U}(3\pi/8).
\]
They are nonnegative and at most $s(v-a/2),s(u-a/2)$.
A support cut at $(-v,1)$ has horizontal legs
$\delta_L/s$ and $\delta_L/(c-s)$. Its whole charged triangle
has area $K\delta_L^2$, where $K=1/k$.
If the latter leg exceeded $a-v$, the cut with
$\delta_0=(c-s)(a-v)$ would already give $D\ge(a-v)^2/r$.
But \cref{eq:short-geometric-comparisons} gives
\[
 a-v>a/2-t_0-h/4>k/3,\qquad (a-v)^2/r>D_*.
\]
This contradicts \cref{eq:short-stability}. The right cut is identical.
They lie in separate wings, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-cut-payment}
 D\ge K(\delta_L^2+\delta_R^2).
\end{equation}

Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \Gamma=Kc^2=\frac{4+3r}{4},\qquad \eta_L=\eta-(1-k)h,\\
 E_L=(\eta_L-\delta_L/c)_+,\qquad E_R=(\eta-\delta_R/c)_+.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The definitions give the exact companion identity
\[
 1-1/c+k^2=\eta,\qquad 1/c+K\eta=2m_*.
\]
At the left zero $x_0=k-v+h$ of the diagonal tent, the actual
$\pi/8$ companion wall has height at least $E_L$.
A point attaining $u$ has $x+y=1+u$ and $y\le1$, and lies on
the unchanged right wing. Its first-wall lower bound is
$1-1/s+(u-x)/k$. This dominates the additional ascending wall
up to $x_0+E_L/(1-k)$, because
\[
 2M_0-h>2m_*-h/2\ge1/c+KE_L.
\]
The two inner tips lie before the diagonal apex. The useful identity is
\[
 m_*-\bigl(k+\eta/(1-k)\bigr)=N-1>h_*/4>h/4.
\]
In the reflected coordinate, an attaining point for $v$ lowered by $h$
supplies the other companion wall. Its condition is
$2M_0-kh>2m_*=1/c+K\eta\ge1/c+KE_R$.
We obtain two disjoint additional triangles of full areas
$\Gamma E_L^2,\Gamma E_R^2$. Each omitted exterior tip has horizontal
length at most $L_0+h/4$, where
\[
 L_0=t_0-k+\eta/k=\eta(1-k)/k=(1+k)\eta.
\]
Thus
\[
 \int_J B\ge N_{45}+\Gamma(E_L^2+E_R^2)-k(L_0+h/4)^2,
\]
where $N_{45}$ is the diagonal-tent integral used above.
This remains valid when one of the peaks is zero.

For $\eta_i,\delta\ge0$, completing a square gives
\[
 K\delta^2+\Gamma(\eta_i-\delta/c)_+^2\ge\Gamma\eta_i^2/2.
\]
Combining this with \cref{eq:short-cut-payment} gives
\[
 P\le2k-kh/2-h^2/8
       -\frac\Gamma2\bigl[(\eta-(1-k)h)^2+\eta^2\bigr]
       +k(L_0+h/4)^2.
\]
Its linear and quadratic coefficients in $h$ are nonpositive, so
\[
 P\le2k-\Gamma\eta^2+kL_0^2
   =2k-(\Gamma-2k)\eta^2<\underline p<\Mc/2.
\]
The strict comparison uses the same generated enclosures as
\cref{eq:short-geometric-comparisons}. This contradicts the high score
and proves the exclusion at the natural unit-width boundary.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Wide caps and the endpoint equations}

For a wide cap, endpoint stationarity forces the diagonal supports
outward. That raises the middle tent. The nearby hallway positions
then either cut two exterior triangles or raise the niche farther.
We first use the diagonal balance to confine the parameters, and
then pay for these two extra triangles.

\begin{lemma}[Wide three-tent bound]\label{lem:preliminary-wide}
Let $8/5\le a<3$, $0\le h<1/2$, and let $U,A,J,B$ satisfy the
geometric hypotheses of \cref{lem:preliminary-short}, without its
upper width restriction. Put $q_\pm=B(\pm a/2)$,
$e_R=A(a)$, and $e_L=A(-a)$. Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preliminary-endpoint-laws}
 e_R=\tfrac12+\tfrac h4+\tfrac{3q_+-q_-}{4},\qquad
 e_L=\tfrac12-\tfrac{3h}{4}+\tfrac{3q_--q_+}{4}.
\end{equation}
Then the score is less than $\underline p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Here use the original supports
$u=rh_U(\pi/4)-1$, $v=rh_U(3\pi/4)-1$,
$m=(u+v)/2$, $w_R=a-u$, and $w_L=a-v$.
The affine roof extension is $L_h(x)=1-h/2+(h/a)x$.
Concavity and the height bound imply
\[
 a/2\le u\le a,\quad a/2-h\le v\le a-3h/2,\quad
 u\ge a+e_R-1,\quad v\ge a+e_L-1.
\]
The majorant $\min(1,L_h,1+u-x,1+v+x)$ has exterior area
\[
 O_0=a-\frac58ah-\frac12
       \left[w_R^2+\frac{(w_L-3h/2)^2}{1-h/a}\right].
\]
The endpoint equations give
$e_R+e_L=1-h/2+(q_++q_-)/2\ge1-h/2$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wide-endpoint-budget}
 w_R+w_L\le1+h/2,\qquad m\ge a-1/2-h/4.
\end{equation}
The diagonal tent has its apex in $J$, and its integral is
\[
 N_{45}=(m-k)^2-\tfrac12
       [(a/2-k-w_R)_+^2+(a/2-k-w_L)_+^2].
\]

Write $b_0=a/2-k$. For $t\ge b_0$ with $3/2-t/a\ge0$,
\[
 w^2-(b_0-w)_+^2\ge2tw-t^2,
\]
\[
 \frac{(w-3h/2)^2}{1-h/a}-(b_0-w)_+^2
 \ge2tw-t^2+(-3t+t^2/a)h.
\]
The second follows from the exact remainder
$[w-t-h(3/2-t/a)]^2/(1-h/a)$; if $w<b_0$, this remainder
is at least $(b_0-w)^2$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wide-relaxation}
 P\le a-2t(a-m)+t^2-(m-k)^2
       -\left[\tfrac58a-\tfrac32t+\frac{t^2}{2a}\right]h.
\end{equation}
For $a\le1+2k$ take $t=1/2$. This yields
\[
 P\le m+1/4-(m-k)^2-D(a)h,\qquad
 D(a)=5a/8-3/4+1/(8a)\ge21/64.
\]
We need the two nearby-angle peaks to fit in $J$, so estimate their
span directly. A hypothetical score at least $\underline p$ first forces
$a\le1+2k$: otherwise take $t=b_0$ in
\cref{eq:wide-relaxation} and use \cref{eq:wide-endpoint-budget} to obtain
$P\le(5/2)k-1/4<41/50<\underline p$.

At the first wide width put
\[
 a_0=8/5,\qquad m_0=a_0-1/2,\qquad d_0=D(a_0),
\]
and let the width price, height price, and available area be
\[
 p_a=2(m_0-k)-1,\qquad p_h=d_0-p_a/4,\qquad
 b=m_0+1/4-(m_0-k)^2-41/50.
\]
The midpoint tangent and endpoint law give these prices.
Indeed, completing the midpoint square gives
\[
 m+1/4-(m-k)^2
 =m_0+1/4-(m_0-k)^2-p_a(m-m_0)-(m-m_0)^2.
\]
Since $p_a>0$ and $m-m_0\ge a-a_0-h/4$, the diagonal area bound and
$P\ge\underline p>41/50$ give one joint budget:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wide-endpoint-price}
 p_a(a-a_0)+p_h\,h\le b.
\end{equation}
Let $\lambda=1-k/2$. The same root enclosures as before give
\[
 p_h>0,\qquad p_a\ge p_h\lambda,\qquad
 b<p_h(rN-\lambda a_0).
\]
For a direct check use $k^2+2k=1$, $k<29/70$, and $N>27/25$;
the last comparison is already strict with $N$ replaced by $27/25$.
Thus the width price pays for its contribution to the peak span, and
\cref{eq:wide-endpoint-price} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wide-stability}
 a\le1+2k,\qquad \lambda a+h<rN=4s.
\end{equation}

Put $z=h/a$ and $q=(v+h/2)/(1-z)$, so $a/2\le q\le a$.
The near-vertical deficits of the majorant are
\[
 \delta_L=c(1-h/2)+(s-cz)q-h_U(5\pi/8),\qquad
 \delta_R=c+su-h_U(3\pi/8).
\]
The middle points show $\delta_L\le(s-cz)(q-a/2)$ and
$\delta_R\le s(u-a/2)$, so the inner half of each support cut fits between the middle endpoint
and the diagonal contact. Since $z<k$, their total exterior payment is
at least
\[
 \frac{\delta_L^2+\delta_R^2}{2kc^2}
 =r(\delta_L^2+\delta_R^2).
\]
Only these inner halves are needed; their outer legs need no fitting check.
The extra left deficit from tilt is
\[
 \tau=(c+sv-c(1-h/2)-(s-cz)q)/c
      =(1-k)h(1/2+q/a)\le\tfrac32(1-k)h.
\]

The $\pi/8$ tent has ascending wall
$b_R+kx$ and descending companion lower bound $B_R-x/k$, where
\[
 b_R=1-1/c+kv-\delta_L/c-\tau,\qquad
 B_R=e_R+a/k-1/s.
\]
In the reflected coordinate the corresponding $\pi/8$-type tent
at $3\pi/8$ has
$b_L=1-1/c+ku-\delta_R/c$ and $B_L=e_L+a/k-1/s$.
Their crossovers with the descending diagonal baselines are
\[
 x_L^R=(u-k-b_R)/r,\quad x_L^L=(v-k-b_L)/r,
\]
and their additional peaks are
\[
 Z_R=(B_R+b_R)/2-u+k,\qquad
 Z_L=(B_L+b_L)/2-v+k.
\]
The crossover offsets from the two diagonal apices are
$km+2s-1+(\delta_L/c+\tau)/r$ and
$km+2s-1+\delta_R/(rc)$, which are positive by
\cref{eq:wide-endpoint-budget}: $m\ge a-1/2-h/4>3/4$.
The middle endpoint support bounds place the tentative peaks in $J$
provided
\[
 e_R+h+\lambda a\le1+4s,\qquad
 e_L+\lambda a\le1+4s.
\]
Both follow at once from $e_R,e_L\le1$, $h\ge0$, and
\cref{eq:wide-stability}. The same diagonal budget therefore places
both extra triangles without separate width or height cuts.

Truncate each half-triangle at its diagonal baseline zero.
Set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 F_R=r(u-k-x_L^R),\quad F_L=r(v-k-x_L^L),\\
 \widehat Z_Q=\min(Z_Q,F_Q),\quad
 \ell_Q=(Z_Q-F_Q)_+\quad(Q=R,L).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The two retained triangles are disjoint, lie in $J$, and add niche
area at least $[(\widehat Z_R)_+^2+(\widehat Z_L)_+^2]/(2r)$.
Their truncation lengths obey
\[
 \begin{split}
 2\ell_R&\le[e_R+2rw_R+h-(3k/2)a-1-4s+2rk]_+,\\
 2\ell_L&\le[e_L+2rw_L-(3k/2)a-1-4s+2rk]_+.
 \end{split}
\]
To bound these expressions we need no box estimate for a partial
niche. Solving \cref{eq:preliminary-endpoint-laws} using
$e_R\le1$, $e_L\le1-3h/2$, and $q_\pm\ge0$ gives directly
\[
 e_R\ge1/3+h/2+(2/3)q_+,\qquad
 e_L\ge1/3-2h/3+(2/3)q_-.
\]
In particular $w_R\le2/3-h/4$ and $w_L\le2/3+3h/4$.
Using also $e_Q\le1-w_Q$, $a\ge8/5$, and
\[
 (2r-1)\tfrac23-\tfrac{12}{5}k-4s+2rk<0
\]
in the displayed truncation bounds gives
\[
 \ell_R\le(5-2r)h/8,\quad
 \ell_L\le3(2r-1)h/8,\quad \ell_R+\ell_L\le h.
\]
The constant's sign follows from $r<10/7$, $k<3/7$, $s>3/8$.

Adding the two peak formulas, using $e_R+e_L\ge1-h/2$,
$\tau\le3(1-k)h/2$, and the last truncation bound, gives
\[
 \widehat Z_R+\widehat Z_L
 \ge Q(a,m,h)-(\delta_L+\delta_R)/(2c),
\]
where the exact demand is
\[
 Q=(2+k)a+(k-2)m+\tfrac32+2k-(3+k)N-(2-3k/4)h.
\]
There is no need to round its coefficients. Put
$D_0=r(\delta_L^2+\delta_R^2)$ and
$G_0=[(\widehat Z_R)_+^2+(\widehat Z_L)_+^2]/(2r)$.
Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 Q_+^2\le(4+5k)(D_0+G_0).
\]
Indeed each retained peak contributes reciprocal price $2r$, and
each normalized support deficit contributes $k/2$; their sum is
$4r+k=4+5k$. Consequently
\[
 P\le m+1/4-(m-k)^2-d_0h-\frac{Q_+^2}{4+5k},
 \qquad d_0=D(a_0).
\]
Choose the common price to pay exactly for the tilt:
\[
 t=\frac{d_0}{2(2-3k/4)}.
\]
The inequality $Q_+^2\ge2(4+5k)tQ-(4+5k)^2t^2$
cancels the height term. Completing the remaining midpoint square
and using $a\ge a_0$ gives
\[
 P\le k+\tfrac12
 -2t\bigl[(2+k)a_0+\tfrac32-\tfrac{3k}{2}-(3+k)N\bigr]
 +(9-k)t^2.
\]
The existing Newton bounds for $k$ and $N\le13/12$ compare this
expression directly with $\underline p$. Thus one price and one
square suffice; no constrained midpoint optimization remains.
This contradiction proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The intermediate tilt bound}

The inclined middle chord also bounds the continuation of the low
wing. Pay its triangular area loss together with the diagonal niche:
one common price cancels the unknown support coordinates. Completing
the price square gives a bound coupling width and tilt.

\begin{lemma}[One-angle tilt estimate]\label{lem:preliminary-tilt}
For $1\le a\le8/5$, $0\le h<1/2$, and the same middle roof,
a barrier dominating the actual diagonal tent gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intermediate-tilt}
 P\le a-\tfrac58ah-\frac{2(a-3h/4-k)^2}{4-h/a}
  =1-\frac{(a-r)^2}{2}
       -\frac{2a}{4a-h}(k+h/2)^2.
\end{equation}
A score at least $\underline p$ consequently forces
\[
 h<1/3,\qquad (a-r)^2+\frac{4a}{4a-h}(k+h/2)^2
 \le2(1-\underline p).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the lifted cap and its diagonal supports $u,v$ from the short
proof. Concavity of the original roof imposes $v\le a-h/2$.
In addition to the horizontal majorant, the lifted left wing lies
below $1+h/2+(h/a)x$. Its cut removes exterior area
\[
 D_{\rm tilt}=\frac{h(v-a/2)^2}{2(a-h)}.
\]
Indeed the first triangle has area
$(h/a)(v-a/2)^2/2$, and the second has this value times
$(h/a)/(1-h/a)$; both are charged because $v\le a-h/2$.
The score is therefore bounded by $F_h(a,u,v)-D_{\rm tilt}$.
We can pay this loss together with the diagonal niche using one price.
Put
\[
 w_R=a-u,\qquad w_L=a-v+h,\qquad m=(u+v-h)/2.
\]
Completing the ceiling square gives the same perspective losses as in
\cref{eq:wide-relaxation}. Apply its two clipped-square inequalities
with a common price $t\ge a/2-k$, and also use
\[
 (m-k)_+^2\ge2t(m-k)-t^2\qquad(t\ge0).
\]
The two support coordinates cancel. We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intermediate-price}
 P\le a-\frac58ah-2t(a-3h/4-k)+\frac{4-h/a}{2}t^2.
\end{equation}
Complete this single price square and choose
\[
 t=\frac{2(a-3h/4-k)}{4-h/a}.
\]
This price is admissible: $0\le t\le a/2$, and
\[
 t-(a/2-k)=\frac{2ak-h(a+k)}{4a-h}\ge0.
\]
For the numerator use $h<1/2$, $a\ge1$, and $k>1/3$:
\[
 2ak-\frac{a+k}{2}
 =(a-1)(2k-1/2)+(3k-1)/2\ge0.
\]
The clipped perspective inequalities need only $t/a\le3/2$, already
supplied by $t\le a/2$. Substitution in \cref{eq:intermediate-price}
proves the first formula in \cref{eq:intermediate-tilt}, without
optimizing either support coordinate. The second follows from
$k^2+2k=1$.

Compare directly with the reference area. Dropping the nonpositive
width square and using $a\le8/5$ gives
\[
 P\le1-\frac{16/5}{32/5-h}(k+h/2)^2.
\]
If $h\ge1/3$, the last loss increases with $h$ and is greater than
its value at $1/3$ with $k$ replaced by $k_-$. The generated root
bounds in \cref{lem:constant-bounds} give the single comparison
\[
 1-\frac{16/5}{32/5-1/3}(k_-+1/6)^2<\underline p.
\]
This proves $h<1/3$. Keeping the width square in
\cref{eq:intermediate-tilt} gives the stated joint area budget.

\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Canonical range]\label{lem:canonical-range}\label{lem:preliminary-cuts}
Every canonical full maximum satisfies
\[
 1/2<C<\frac45,\qquad
 |f_U(C)-f_U(-C)|<\frac13,
 \quad I_U=[-2C,2C].
\]
The same conclusions hold for every canonical proper joint maximum
whose angle exceeds $\arctan(8/3)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The reference competitor and \cref{lem:constant-bounds} give
score $M/2>\underline p$. Width coercivity gives $a=2C<3$.
The positive-pressure and top-localization results give height one,
an affine middle roof, and lower middle height greater than $1/2$.
Orient its higher middle endpoint to the right. In the proper case
reflect the actual barrier as well. It remains nonnegative and
dominates all three tents, since
$\arctan(8/3)>3\pi/8$. The endpoint equations are precisely
\cref{eq:preliminary-endpoint-laws} after relabeling.
Apply \cref{lem:preliminary-short,lem:preliminary-wide} to obtain
$1<a<8/5$, then \cref{lem:preliminary-tilt} to obtain
the height-drop bound.
\end{proof}
