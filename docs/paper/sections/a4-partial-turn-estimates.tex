\section{Estimates for the terminal tail}\label{app:partial-turn-estimates}

\subsection{The initial-angle exclusion}

At a small terminal angle, the outgoing wall alone removes enough
area. For the remaining angles we retain that wall together with the
visited diagonal pair. Their outer supports bound the reward, and
their inner walls bound the penalty. Integrating fixed pieces of these
walls gives supporting planes; two square completions bound the relaxation.

\begin{proposition}\label{app:partial-initial}
For every downward cap $U$ and $\pi/4\le\alpha\le\arctan(8/3)$,
\[
 P_\alpha(U)<\frac\Mc2.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First put $\kappa=\cot\alpha$ and center the projection as
$I=[-2C,2C]$. Write $A=f_U$ and
$H=h_U(\alpha)/\sin\alpha$. The roof satisfies $A(x)\le H-\kappa x$
and the whole terminal wall is $H-\csc\alpha-\kappa x$.
The left exterior roof is at most one; integrate the support bound
on $[C,2C]$ and the terminal lower bound on $[-C,0]$, even where
that affine bound is negative. This gives
\[
 P_\alpha\le(1+\sqrt{1+\kappa^2})C-2\kappa C^2
 \le\frac{(1+\sqrt{1+\kappa^2})^2}{8\kappa}.
\]
On $4/5\le\kappa\le1$ the last expression decreases: its derivative
has the sign of $(\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-2)(\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}+1)$.
Its largest value is $(33+5\sqrt{41})/80<\underline p$.
This handles $\alpha\le\arctan(5/4)$.

\paragraph{The support data forced by the passage}

\Cref{lem:selection-canonical,lem:selection-maximum} supplies height extrusion, middle-chord reduction, coercivity, attainment, and top insertion. The attainment proof also applies with
\(\alpha\) fixed: if a cap has score greater than \(\underline p\), the
fixed-angle maximum is attained inside the same compact width interval.
Indeed the width-six calculation in \cref{lem:selection-width}
is below $3/4$, so every cap with score above $\underline p$ has
width below six. A maximizing sequence at this fixed angle can
therefore be centered in the same compact box; score continuity
and Blaschke compactness give its maximum.
Its canonical representative has height one, an affine middle roof, and
a top face meeting the middle window. Thus, a hypothetical cap with score
at least \(\Mc/2\) yields such a canonical fixed-angle maximizer
with at least the same score. It suffices to rule out that maximizer.


Center the projection and introduce the parameters

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-2}
\begin{gathered}
I=[-2C,2C],\quad J=[-C,C],\\
K=\sqrt2-1,\quad \kappa=\cot\alpha,\quad
d=\frac{\kappa}{1+\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

The outgoing-wall estimate above handles
\(\alpha\le\arctan(5/4)\). For the new interval it suffices to take

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-3}
\begin{gathered}
\frac38\le\kappa\le\frac45,\qquad
\kappa d=\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-1,\\
\kappa=\frac{2d}{1-d^2},\qquad 0<d<K.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

Use the actual supports to define

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-4}
u=\max_U(x+y)-1,\quad v=\max_U(-x+y)-1,\quad
b=\max_U\left(x+\frac{y-1}{\kappa}\right).
\end{equation}

The three outer support lines are
\(1+u-x\), \(1+v+x\), and \(1-\kappa(x-b)\). The actual outgoing
inner wall is \(\kappa(b-d-x)\). Consequently

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-5}
\begin{aligned}
A(x)&\le \min\{1,1+u-x,1+v+x,1-\kappa(x-b)\},\\
N_\alpha(U)(x)&\ge
\max\{0,\min(u-K-x,v-K+x),\kappa(b-d-x)\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The 45-degree angle is genuinely visited throughout the new interval.
Only the displayed lower bound on the true niche is used.

There is an essential compatibility condition between the two first
supports:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-6}
\boxed{b\le u\le 2(1-\kappa)C+\kappa b,
\qquad u,v,b\le2C.}
\end{equation}

Indeed, \(y\le1\) and \(\kappa<1\) give
\(x+(y-1)/\kappa\le x+y-1\), hence \(b\le u\). At a point attaining
\(u\),

\[
\kappa b\ge\kappa x+y-1=u-(1-\kappa)x
\ge u-2(1-\kappa)C.
\]

The remaining upper bounds follow directly from the containing box.

Since the middle is affine and its top meets \(J\), either
\(A(-C)=1\) or \(A(C)=1\). The first alternative gives
\(v\ge C\) and \(u,b\ge-C\); the second gives \(u,b\ge C\) and
\(v\ge-C\). These alternatives will be handled directly.

\paragraph{The natural diagonal width}

Put
\[
g_C(z)=\int_C^{2C}(x-z)_+\,dx
=\frac{(2C-z)_+^2-(C-z)_+^2}{2}.
\]
For the diagonal comparison only, reflect the cap if necessary so that
\(v\in[C,2C]\). Dropping the terminal wall gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-7}
P_\alpha\le F_{45}(u,v):=2C-g_C(u)-g_C(v)
-\int_{-C}^C[\min(u-K-x,v-K+x)]_+\,dx.
\end{equation}
The diagonal tent and the two exterior losses give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-9}
F_{45}\le\max\{2/3,\ 2C-2(C-K/2)^2\}.
\end{equation}
Here is a direct way to check the clipping. A nonpositive apex, or an
apex to the left of the window, gives the first bound from the exterior
losses alone. Otherwise, if \(u\ge C\), integrate the positive tent
and use the square supporting inequality
\(z_+^2\ge2qz-q^2\), \(q\ge0\), at the balanced supports
\(u=v=C+K/2\). If \(u<C\), the first wall comparison below, with
\(\kappa=0\), gives the same second bound. If \(2C<K\), use
\(P_\alpha\le2C<K<2/3\) instead.

For \(C\le K\), the quadratic in \eqref{eq:partial-at-9} increases
with \(C\), and its endpoint value is
\[
2K-K^2/2=3K-1/2<3/4<\underline p.
\]
Thus the angle exclusion needs only \(C>K\).
For later use, derive the width needed to separate the terminal corner.
Its horizontal displacement is at least \(3C\), and its height
increase is at most one, so the required inequality is
\(3C\sin\alpha>1+\cos\alpha\). Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-10}
\begin{gathered}
\alpha_0=\arctan(8/3),\qquad\kappa_0=\cot\alpha_0=3/8,\\
C_{\rm corner}=\frac{1+\cos\alpha_0}{3\sin\alpha_0}
=\frac{\sqrt{1+\kappa_0^2}+\kappa_0}{3}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The diagonal bound already excludes \(C\le C_{\rm corner}\).
Indeed one Newton step from \(9\) bounds \(\sqrt{73}\), while the
two steps defining \(k_+\) bound \(K\). With
\(C_+=(\mathcal N_{73}(9)+3)/24\), direct substitution gives
\[
2C_{\rm corner}-2(C_{\rm corner}-K/2)^2
\le2C_+-2(C_+-k_+/2)^2<\underline p.
\]
Both comparisons increase with their parameters in the displayed range.
Consequently every competitive canonical cap has
\(C>C_{\rm corner}\). For \(\alpha\ge\alpha_0\), increasing the angle
increases its sine and decreases its cosine, so this same width gives
\(3C\sin\alpha>1+\cos\alpha\). The width cut is therefore exactly
what the corner geometry asks for.

\paragraph{Reducing to one support domain}

For \(b\le u\), write

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-14}
\begin{gathered}
x_0=\frac{u-\kappa b}{1-\kappa},\\
\begin{aligned}
Q_C(u,b)&=\int_C^{2C}\max\{0,x-u,\kappa(x-b)\}\,dx\\
&=\kappa g_C(b)+(1-\kappa)g_C(x_0).
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

The identity follows from \(b\le u\le x_0\): the three successive
active pieces are zero, \(\kappa(x-b)\), and \(x-u\), with any
pieces outside \([C,2C]\) automatically clipped by \(g_C\).

Define

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-15}
\begin{aligned}
N(u,v,b)&=\int_{-C}^C
\max\{0,\min(u-K-x,v-K+x),\kappa(b-d-x)\}\,dx,\\
F(u,v,b)&=2C-g_C(v)-Q_C(u,b)-N(u,v,b).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

In either high-endpoint alternative the left exterior majorant in \eqref{eq:partial-at-5}
is \(\min(1,1+v+x)\), and the right exterior majorant is
\(1-\max(0,x-u,\kappa(x-b))\). Thus
\(P_\alpha\le F\).

Suppose first that the high endpoint is on the right and \(v<C\).
Increase \(v\) to \(C\), keeping \(u,b\) fixed. This is a variation
of the relaxation; it need not preserve realizability by a cap. The
charged left exterior reward increases by \(C\) times the support
increment. Subtracting the terminal line leaves a positive asymmetric
tent. Its complete-area increase bounds the change of the clipped niche,
and is at most the final rising-wall exposure length times that increment. Put

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-16}
p=\frac{u-v}{2},\quad e=b-d,\quad
x_L=\frac{\kappa e-v+K}{1+\kappa}.
\end{equation}

The rising wall of that complete tent runs from \(x_L\) to \(p\),
so its length is \((p-x_L)_+\). Since \(u\le2C\), \(v\le C\),
and \(b\ge C\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-17}
p-x_L\le
\frac{(3-\kappa)C+2\kappa d-2K}{2(1+\kappa)}<C.
\end{equation}

The strict inequality uses \(\kappa>1/3\) and \(\kappa d<K\).
The exterior reward therefore pays for every finite niche increase,
and \(F\) increases until \(v=C\). All of \eqref{eq:partial-at-6} is
preserved. It is therefore enough to bound \(F\) on the convex domain

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-18}
\boxed{D_C=\{C\le v\le2C,\ -C\le b\le2C,
\ b\le u\le2(1-\kappa)C+\kappa b\}.}
\end{equation}

This also contains the original high-left alternative. No reflection of
the partial objective has been used.

\paragraph{Two wall comparisons}

Write
\[
a=u-K,\quad c=v-K,\quad e=b-d,\quad p=(u-v)/2,\qquad
Y=(1+\kappa)C-c+\kappa e.
\]
If the diagonal apex is nonpositive, or lies to the left of \(J\),
the exterior losses already give \(F\le2/3\), as above. We may
therefore assume \(u+v\ge2K\) and \(p\in J\).

The falling diagonal line is the only concave part of the wall maximum.
Separate it with the pointwise inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-wall-separation}
\max(B,L)-(B-A)_++(-A)_+
\le\max\{0,\min(A,B),L\},\qquad A+B\ge0.
\end{equation}
To verify it, split at \(A=B\) and \(A=0\). With
\(A=a-x\), \(B=c+x\), and \(L=\kappa(e-x)\), integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-niche-separation}
N\ge V-(C-p)^2+\int_{-C}^C(x-a)_+\,dx,
\qquad V=\int_{-C}^C\max(c+x,\kappa(e-x))\,dx.
\end{equation}
The negative term is the area swept out by moving the tent apex.
The last term retains the falling wall's floor loss.

The useful supporting planes come from integrating a chosen affine
branch over a fixed interval. For any \(q\in[C,2C]\),
\[
g_C(z)\ge\int_q^{2C}(x-z)\,dx.
\]
The balanced diagonal support is \(C+K/2\). Use \(q=C\) for the terminal ramp and \(q=C+K/2\) for the diagonal
ramp in \eqref{eq:partial-at-14}. In \(V\), use the terminal line on
\([-C,K/2-C]\) and the rising diagonal line on \([K/2-C,C]\).
These choices give, for every position of the actual switches,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-fixed-planes}
\begin{aligned}
Q_C&\ge\frac{3C^2}{2}-Cu
 +\frac K2\bigl(u-\kappa b-(1-\kappa)C\bigr)
 -\frac{(1-\kappa)K^2}{8},\\
V&\ge2Cc+\frac K2\bigl((1+\kappa)C-c+\kappa e\bigr)
 -\frac{(1+\kappa)K^2}{8}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The split positions balance exterior and niche exposure. Define their
algebraic center and comparison value by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-25}
\begin{gathered}
b_*=(K+d)/2,\qquad
u_*=(1-\kappa)C+K/2+\kappa d/2,\\
v_*=(1+\kappa)C+K/2-\kappa d/2,\\
F_*=2C-2(C-K/2)^2-\kappa K(C-d/2).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
These formulas only center square identities; the point need not
represent a cap or have any particular ordering of its wall intersections.

First suppose \(a\le C\). The apex assumptions imply \(a\ge-C\),
so the last integral in \eqref{eq:initial-niche-separation} is
\((C-a)^2/2\). Also \(g_C(v)=(2C-v)^2/2\).
Adding \eqref{eq:initial-fixed-planes} and completing the square gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-first-square}
F\le F_*-\frac{(u+v-2C-K)^2}{4}\le F_*.
\end{equation}

Now suppose \(a\ge C\) and \(Y\ge0\). Compatibility places
\(x_0\) in \([C,2C]\); it also gives
\(Y\le2(1+\kappa)C\). Thus retain the two exact switch triangles:
\[
Q_C\ge\kappa(3C^2/2-Cb)
 +\frac{(2(1-\kappa)C-u+\kappa b)^2}{2(1-\kappa)},
\qquad
V=2Cc+\frac{Y^2}{2(1+\kappa)}.
\]
Set \(\xi=u-u_*\), \(\eta=v-v_*\), \(\zeta=b-b_*\), and
\(T=C+K-u_*\). Substitution into
\eqref{eq:initial-niche-separation}, dropping its nonnegative last
term, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-second-square}
\begin{split}
2(F_*-F)\ge{}&2T\xi-T^2+\eta^2\\
&+\frac{(\xi-\kappa\zeta)^2}{1-\kappa}
 +\frac{(\eta-\kappa\zeta)^2}{1+\kappa}
 -\frac{(\xi-\eta)^2}{2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here \(T\ge0\), because \(C\ge K>d/2\), and \(\xi\ge T\),
because \(u\ge C+K\). The last three terms are nonnegative by
weighted Cauchy--Schwarz: the two denominator weights sum to two.
The right side is nonnegative, so \(F\le F_*\).

We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-30}
\boxed{F\le F_*\quad\text{whenever }a\le C\text{ or }Y\ge0.}
\end{equation}
Only the case \(a>C\), \(Y<0\) remains.

\paragraph{The remaining domain pays a larger diagonal tent}

Suppose \(u>C+K\) and \(Y<0\). Combining the latter with \eqref{eq:partial-at-6} gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-31}
v-u>(3\kappa-1)C+K-\kappa d>0.
\end{equation}

In particular \(u,v>C+K\), so both ends of the 45-degree tent are
strictly positive. Its apex belongs to \((-C,C)\), since
\(u,v\in(C+K,2C]\). Dropping the outgoing support and charge only
enlarges the relaxation. With \(h=(u+v)/2\), exact 45-degree
integration therefore gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-32}
F\le 2C-2C^2+2KC-(h-C)^2.
\end{equation}

Moreover, \eqref{eq:partial-at-31}, \(C\ge K\), and
\(\kappa d<K\) imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-33}
\begin{gathered}
h-C>\frac{(3\kappa-1)C+3K-\kappa d}{2}
>\frac{(1+3\kappa)K}{2}\ge g,\\
g=\frac{(1+3\kappa_0)K}{2}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus the gap comes from the diagonal offset and the angular endpoint.
Finally, \(1+K=\sqrt2\) gives
\(2(1+K)C-2C^2\le1\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-34}
\boxed{F<1-g^2<\underline p.}
\end{equation}
For the last comparison use the lower Newton enclosure \(K>k_-\)
from \eqref{eq:preliminary-constants}; substituting its defining formula
and the formula for \(\underline p\) is an exact rational check.

This completes all parameter and clipping cases in \(D_C\).

\paragraph{Completing the width square}

Completing the square in \(C\) in \eqref{eq:partial-at-30} gives the unrestricted bound

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-35}
F_*\le B(\kappa):=
\frac12+\frac K2+\frac K2\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}
-\frac{\kappa(1-K)}2+\frac{\kappa^2K^2}{8}.
\end{equation}

The maximizing width for this quadratic is
\(C=1/2+K(2-\kappa)/4\); no assumption that a cap realizes the
critical supports or this width is required for the upper bound.
On \([3/8,4/5]\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-36}
\begin{aligned}
B'(\kappa)
&=\frac{K\kappa}{2\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}}
-\frac{1-K}{2}+\frac{\kappa K^2}{4}\\
&\le\frac{2K}{5}-\frac{1-K}{2}+\frac{K^2}{5}
=\frac{5K-3}{10}<0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Hence the largest value occurs at \(\kappa=3/8\), where

\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-37}
\begin{aligned}
B(3/8)&=b(K,\sqrt{73}),\\
b(K,s)&=\frac{(2-3/8)^2}{8}
 +K\left[\frac{1+3/8}{2}-\frac{(3/8)^2}{4}+\frac{s}{16}\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This endpoint can be compared directly with the reference area. For $q>0$
and $x>0$, write $\mathcal N_q(x)=(x+q/x)/2$. Squaring shows
$\sqrt q\le\mathcal N_q(x)$, with strict inequality unless $x=\sqrt q$.
Use two steps from $3/2$ for $\sqrt2$ and one step from $9$ for $\sqrt{73}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-at-38}
 B(3/8)
 <b\bigl(\mathcal N_2(\mathcal N_2(3/2))-1,
          \mathcal N_{73}(9)\bigr)
 <\underline p<\Mc/2.
\end{equation}
The middle inequality follows by substituting the Newton formulas and
\cref{eq:reference-half-area-lower}.

All other cases were bounded strictly below \(\underline p\).
The earlier outgoing-only theorem covers
\([\pi/4,\arctan(5/4)]\) by a value below \(\underline p\).
Fixed-angle attainment and the canonical reduction therefore prove the
asserted universal inequality.

\end{proof}

\subsection{A width cut with the actual partial barrier}

\begin{proposition}\label{app:partial-width}
A canonical proper joint maximum has $C<37/50$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:preliminary-cuts}, it suffices to exclude
$37/50\le C<4/5$ and $0\le h=h_L+h_R<1/3$.
Relabel the high middle endpoint as the right one, reflecting both
the cap and its actual barrier if necessary. Put $a=2C$ and let
$q_\pm$ be the values of this relabeled barrier at $\pm a/2$.
The endpoint equations and roof concavity give
\[
 e_R=\tfrac12+\tfrac h4+(3q_+-q_-)/4\le1,\qquad
 e_L=\tfrac12-\tfrac{3h}4+(3q_--q_+)/4\le1-3h/2.
\]
Use the upper bound on $e_L$ in the first equation, and the upper
bound on $e_R$ in the second, to obtain
\[
 e_R\ge\tfrac13+\tfrac h2+\tfrac23q_+,\qquad
 e_L\ge\tfrac13-\tfrac{2h}3+\tfrac23q_-.
\]
Lift the low wing by $h$, and use the diagonal parameters $u,v$
and the functions $F_h,S,G_h$ of \eqref{eq:three-tent-objective}.
Write $r=\sqrt2$, $k=r-1$, $c_8=\cos(\pi/8)$,
$\gamma=1-r/2$ and $t=2c_8-r$. The outer deficits
$w_R=a-u$, $w_L=a-v+h$ consequently satisfy
\[
 0\le w_R\le\tfrac23-\tfrac h2,\quad
 0\le w_L\le\tfrac23+\tfrac{2h}3,\quad
 w_R+w_L\le1+\tfrac h2.
\]
The last inequality uses $e_R+e_L\ge1-h/2$.

Here the three-angle calculation must retain the floor. The exterior
support triangles and the two extra tent triangles are exactly those
in the proof of \cref{lem:full-three-tent-score}: all their angles
are visited, and the actual barrier dominates their tents. A triangle
whose signed peak is $Z$ loses, when clipped by the zero floor, at
most $Z_-^2/r$. Completing the same two squares in the exterior
support deficits therefore gives
\[
 P_\alpha(U)\le G_h(a,u,v)+
 \gamma\bigl[(w_R-t)_+^2+(w_L-t)_+^2\bigr].
\]
This derivation uses no upper bound on $q_\pm$ and applies to the
relabeled actual barrier. The convex function in brackets attains
its maximum over the displayed polygon on the sum boundary.
Its endpoints are
$(2/3-h/2,1/3+h)$ and $(1/3-h/6,2/3+2h/3)$.
The second pair is more spread out and has the larger value;
since $1/3-h/6<t$, that value is $(D+2h/3)^2$, where
$D=2/3-t$.

Set $a_0=37/25$, $A=1-k$ and
$\sigma_0=(3r-1)a_0/2-(4c_8-2)$.
The supporting-plane estimate of \cref{lem:full-three-tent-critical}
now gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_\alpha(U)\le B(a_0)-kh/2-h^2/8
       -\tfrac29(\sigma_0-Ah/4)^2+\gamma(D+2h/3)^2,
 \\
 B(a)=a-(a-k)^2/2.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The proper row of \cref{lem:triangle-tilt-payment} bounds this
expression at $h=0$: the direct tilt loss pays for moving the
stationary support and the single floor square together.
At zero tilt its value is the proper row of
\cref{lem:triangle-newton-corners}, so
\[
 P_\alpha(U)<\underline p<\Mc/2.
\]
This contradicts the reference competitor.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The circle gap available for curvature excess}

The terminal wall must exceed the companion's unit-curvature envelope
over the reserve interval. The worst point is its right endpoint.
Writing the circle difference as an average of its slope exposes the
convexity. A single tangent comparison pays for widening the chord
and shifting its center; then only the two width endpoints need checking. The small retained margin will pay for curvature excess.

Put $q=\cos\alpha_0$, $S=\sin\alpha_0$ and
$\mathcal A_q(y)=q^{-1}\int_{y-q/2}^{y+q/2}x/\sqrt{1-x^2}\,\dd x$.
Retain the actual endpoint gap
\[
 \Gamma=\min_{C\in\{1/2,37/50\}}
 \left[\frac{2C-q}{S}-\mathcal A_q(C-q^3/8)\right].
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{app:partial-circle}
For the residual data of \eqref{eq:partial-tail-data}, and
$0\le z\le w$,
\[
 a_\alpha(-C+z)-
 \left[\sqrt{1-(C-T_L)^2}-\sqrt{1-(C+z)^2}\right]
 \ge\Gamma c.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $g(x)=\sqrt{1-x^2}$ and $K(x)=x/g(x)$.
The wall decreases with $z$ and the circle difference increases,
so it suffices to take $z=w=c-d$. Since $T_R\ge0$ and
$T_L\le d$, the gap there is at least
\[
 \frac cs(2C-c)-[g(C-d)-g(C+c-d)].
\]
Also $d\ge\delta=c/(1+s)$, since $H\le1$.
The bracket decreases with $d$. Put
\[
 \eta=\delta-c/2=\frac{c^3}{2(1+s)^2}\ge c^3/8,
 \qquad \mathcal A_q(y)=\frac1q
       \int_{y-q/2}^{y+q/2}K(x)\,\dd x.
\]
The gap divided by $c$ is at least
$(2C-c)/s-\mathcal A_c(C-\eta)$.
On all these intervals $K$ is increasing and convex. Hence the
average increases with its center and width and is convex in its
center. For width monotonicity, pair the positive and negative integration
variables after rescaling to $[-1/2,1/2]$ and apply convexity. A linear function of
$C$ minus a fixed-width average is concave, so its minimum on
$[1/2,37/50]$ occurs at an endpoint.

The terminal-angle cut gives $c\le q:=\cos\alpha_0$. Put
\[
 q^2=\frac{\kappa_0^2}{1+\kappa_0^2},\qquad
 S^2=\frac1{1+\kappa_0^2},\qquad y_r=C-r^3/8.
\]
We compare every chord with the chord of width $q$.
All endpoints lie below $37/50+3/16$; squaring gives $K\le3$ there.
When the center increases from $x$ to $y$, the left circle height
decreases and the right height drops by at most $3(y-x)$.
The height-difference formula for the average therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal A_q(y)-\mathcal A_q(x)\le\frac3q(y-x).
\]
Widening the chord and then paying for its center shift yields
\[
 \mathcal A_c(y_c)\le\mathcal A_q(y_c)
 \le\mathcal A_q(y_q)+\frac{3(q^3-c^3)}{8q}
 \le\mathcal A_q(y_q)+\frac{9q}{8}(q-c).
\]
The tangent to the unit circle at $(q,S)$ says $qc+Ss\le1$.
Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{2C-c}{s}
 &\ge\frac{(2C-c)S}{1-qc}\\
 &=\frac{2C-q}{S}
   +\frac{(q-c)(1-2Cq)}{S(1-qc)}\\
 &\ge\frac{2C-q}{S}+(q-c)(1-2Cq).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Its gain pays for shifting the center, since
$1-2Cq>9q/8$ for $C\le3/4$ and $q<3/8$.
Thus the whole cosine range reduces to one comparison:
\[
 \frac{2C-c}{s}-\mathcal A_c(C-\eta)
 \ge\frac{2C-q}{S}-\mathcal A_q(C-q^3/8).
\]

The last expression is concave in $C$, so its minimum is $\Gamma$.
We need only check that this gap pays for the cubic curvature overlap.
Use one root procedure for both endpoints: let
\[
 U(v)=\mathcal N_v^{\circ4}(1),\qquad L(v)=v/U(v).
\]
Then $L(v)\le\sqrt v\le U(v)$, by the Newton square identity.
Put $q_+=U(q^2)$, $q_-=L(q^2)$ and $S_+=U(S^2)$, with $S_-=L(S^2)$.
At either width endpoint set $y_+=C-(q^2/8)q_+$.
Using $q_+$ moves the left coordinate left and the right coordinate right.
Their circle heights therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{2C-q}{S}&-\mathcal A_q(C-q^3/8)\\
 &\ge\frac{2C-q_+}{S_+}
 -\frac{U(1-(y_+-q_+/2)^2)-L(1-(y_++q_+/2)^2)}{q_-}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Substitution of the four Newton updates at $C=1/2,37/50$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal-gap-payment}
 \Gamma>
 \frac{(4/5)(2/3)^3(3/8)^2}{6(2/3)S_-}>0.
\end{equation}
The quantity on the right is exactly the cubic overlap price required
below. The error estimate below is strict, so this available gap suffices.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reflected supports and the terminal impulse}

Read the companion support backwards from the terminal angle. The missing
angular interval gives a free rotation, and the terminal facet gives one
upward impulse. The first zero of $e-Dr+r^2/2$ is
$D-\sqrt{D^2-2e}$. For $S>2$, an energy bound $Q+1\le S$ gives
excess height at most $S-2$; solving $\ell/2+\ell^2/4=S-2$ gives
duration $\ell=\sqrt{4S-7}-1$. Comparing the sum of these two times
accounts for changes that advance the crossing but lengthen the decay.
Write $C_*=37/50$ and $ k_*=3/8$ for the previously proved width
and terminal-slope cuts.

\begin{lemma}[Crossing and decay comparison]\label{lem:joint-stopping-corner}
For fixed $D,e,\eta$, write
\[
 A=\sqrt{D^2-2e},\qquad
 S=\sqrt{D^2-e(1-e)+\eta},\qquad R=\sqrt{4S-7},
\]
and put $W(D,e,\eta)=D-A+R-1$.
Suppose
\[
 2-2^{-6}\le D\le D_*\le3C_*+ k_*/4,\qquad
 0\le e\le e_*\le1,\qquad0\le\eta\le k_*^2/4.
\]
If $D_*^2-1/4+\eta\ge4$, then
$W(D,e,\eta)\le W(D_*,e_*,\eta)$.
In particular, for $2\le D\le111/50$ and $0\le e\le3/4$,
\[
 W(D,e,0)<2/5+1/3=11/15.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First increase the width while holding $e,\eta$ fixed. Throughout the box,
squaring gives $A\ge11/8$, $S\le7/3$, and $3/4\le R\le8/5$.
For a width increase $\Delta\ge0$, differences of squares give
\[
 A(D+\Delta)-A(D)\le2\Delta,\qquad
 S(D+\Delta)-S(D)\ge\frac45\Delta,
\]
and
\[
 R(D+\Delta)-R(D)
 =\frac{4[S(D+\Delta)-S(D)]}{R(D+\Delta)+R(D)}
 \ge\frac54[S(D+\Delta)-S(D)]\ge\Delta.
\]
Thus the decay gain pays for the change of the first-zero time:
$\Delta-2\Delta+\Delta=0$ suffices.

At width $D_*$ every energy is at least four, because
$e(1-e)\le1/4$. Hence $S\ge2$ and $R\ge1$ for every height.
Also $S^2-A^2=e+e^2+\eta\ge0$, so $S\ge A$.
Compare heights $e\le E$ at this fixed width and write lower-case
$a,s,r$ for the roots at $e$, and $A,S,R$ for those at $E$.
The same difference-of-squares identities give
\[
 W(D_*,E,\eta)-W(D_*,e,\eta)
 =\frac{2(E-e)}{a+A}
   +\frac{4(E-e)(E+e-1)}{(s+S)(r+R)}\ge0.
\]
Indeed $(s+S)(r+R)\ge2(a+A)$ and $E+e-1\ge-1$.
This proves the corner comparison without a height subdivision.
At $(D_*,e_*)=(111/50,3/4)$, substitution in
$e_*-D_*t+t^2/2$ gives a first zero below $2/5$.
The energy is below $(2+(1/3)/2+(1/3)^2/4)^2$, giving
$R-1<1/3$. These two checks prove the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One angular stopping budget]\label{lem:angular-stopping-budget}
Use the zero-slope span and pressure bounds
$D_0=3C_*$ and $e_0=1/2+(3/4)(1/3)=3/4$.
Put $B_0=11/15$ and
\[
 b( k)=(2C_* k-1/3)_+,\qquad
 B( k)=B_0+2 k/3.
\]
Suppose $0\le k\le k_*$, $2-2^{-6}\le D\le D_0+ k/4$,
$0\le e\le1$, $0\le\eta\le k^2/4$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angular-parameter-budget}
 \tfrac32(D-D_0)_+
 +\tfrac9{10}(e-e_0-\tfrac34b( k))_+\le3 k/8.
\end{equation}
Then $W(D,e,\eta)\le B( k)$ and $R-1<3/5$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First increase $D$ to $\bar D=\max(D,D_0)$, then $e$ to
$\bar e=\max(e,e_0)$. Since $D_0^2-1/4>4$,
\cref{lem:joint-stopping-corner} shows that these changes increase $W$.
Put $\Delta D=\bar D-D_0$, $\Delta e=\bar e-e_0$.
We price the change of the whole stopping time by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angular-stopping-price}
 W(\bar D,\bar e,\eta)-W(D_0,e_0,0)
 \le\tfrac32\Delta D+\tfrac9{10}\Delta e+\tfrac25\eta.
\end{equation}
Here is the difference-of-squares calculation behind these prices.
Let $A_0,S_0,R_0$ be the baseline roots, $A,S,R$ the new roots,
and $A_1=\sqrt{\bar D^2-2e_0}$. Squaring gives
$A,A_1\ge5/3$ and $A_1\le6\bar D/7$, with the analogous
bound for $A_0$. Therefore
\[
 A_1-A_0\ge\tfrac76\Delta D,\qquad
 A_1-A\le\tfrac35\Delta e.
\]
For the decay, $S\ge S_0$, $R\ge R_0$, and
\[
 (R-R_0)(R+R_0)(S+S_0)
 =4[(\bar D+D_0)\Delta D+(\bar e+e_0-1)\Delta e+\eta].
\]
The baseline root product satisfies
\[
 S_0R_0\ge\tfrac35(2D_0+ k_*/4),\qquad S_0R_0\ge5/2.
\]
These inequalities are checked by the same Newton enclosures below.
Using $(R+R_0)(S+S_0)\ge4S_0R_0$ and
$\bar e+e_0-1\le3/4$, the last identity gives
\[
 R-R_0\le\tfrac53\Delta D+\tfrac3{10}\Delta e+\tfrac25\eta.
\]
Add the crossing and decay changes to obtain
\eqref{eq:angular-stopping-price}.

Now $\Delta D=(D-D_0)_+$ and
$\Delta e\le\tfrac34b( k)+(e-e_0-\tfrac34b( k))_+$.
The parameter budget consequently gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
 W(D,e,\eta)\le W_0+J( k),\qquad W_0=W(D_0,e_0,0),\\
 J( k)=3 k/8+\tfrac9{10}\tfrac34b( k)+\tfrac25 k^2/4.
\end{gathered}
\]
The function $J$ is convex. Thus it suffices to compare
$W_0+J( k)$ with the line $B( k)$ at $0$ and $ k_*$.
Both comparisons use one evaluation of the baseline.
Write $\mathcal U_x(q)=\mathcal N_q^{\circ3}(x)$ and
$\mathcal L_x(q)=q/\mathcal U_x(q)$ for three Newton steps and
 their reciprocal lower bound. They give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-newton-duration}
 W_0\le\widehat W_0
 :=D_0-\mathcal L_2(D_0^2-3/2)
   +\mathcal U_1(4\mathcal U_2(D_0^2-3/16)-7)-1.
\end{equation}
Inserting the Newton formulas verifies
\[
 \widehat W_0<B_0,\qquad
 \widehat W_0+J( k_*)<B_0+2 k_*/3.
\]
Convexity now gives the deadline throughout $[0, k_*]$.
Finally the common energy bound is
\[
 D^2-e(1-e)+\eta
 \le(D_0+ k_*/4)^2+ k_*^2/4
 <[2+(3/5)/2+(3/5)^2/4]^2.
\]
The defining equation for the decay length gives $R-1<3/5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{app:partial-reflection}
For a residual canonical proper joint maximum, the error in
\eqref{eq:partial-weighted-error} satisfies $\mathcal E_\theta<\Gamma c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a rising middle use the left-wing companion surrogate of
\cref{lem:partial-moment}. For a descending middle use the
right-wing first-support surrogate: it equals the actual first
support on every visited angle and removes the unused middle atom.
Saturation gives its unused tail at $(C,1-h_R)$.
In every case the companion unused tail is
$G(t)=2C\sin t+e_L\cos t$ for $\alpha<t<L$.
Set $\varepsilon=L-\alpha$, $ k=\cot\alpha$, $\delta=c/(1+s)$,
and reflect the support equations, without reflecting their spatial objective:
\[
 P(r)=-q(L-r),\quad Q(r)=-p(L-r),\quad
 U(r)=v(L-r),\quad V(r)=u(L-r).
\]
The curvature and shadow laws give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-reflected-controls}
 \begin{gathered}
 P'=U-1-Q,\quad Q'=V-1+P,\quad P\le1,\ Q\ge-1,\\
 0\le U\le\kappa(Q),\quad0\le V\le\kappa(P),\\
 P,Q>0\Rightarrow U=0,\ V\le1/2,\qquad
 P,Q<0\Rightarrow V=0,\ U\le1/2.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In the final removed interval of the rising surrogate both
curvatures are zero directly; no arm law is needed there.
The initial values are $(P(0),Q(0))=(e,D-1)$, where
\[
 (e,D)=
 \begin{cases}
 (e_L,3C+T_R),&\text{rising or horizontal middle},\\
 (e_L+h_R,3C),&\text{descending middle}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Both densities vanish on $(0,\varepsilon)$.
At $\varepsilon$, $P$ is continuous and $Q$ jumps upwards by
$j=\tau/s\le\delta$. There is no companion terminal atom or
later visited atom. The free initial trajectory is
\[
 P=1+(e-1)\cos r-D\sin r,\qquad
 Q+1=D\cos r+(e-1)\sin r.
\]

We retain the endpoint pressure and outgoing wall together.
Let $H$ be the actual terminal tail height, $h=1-H$, and
$d_\alpha=(1-Hs)/c$ its actual inset. The pressure law gives
\[
 e\le\tfrac12+\tfrac54h+\tfrac34
      \max\{1/3, k(2C+T_R-d_\alpha)\}.
\]
Here the ordinary endpoint box is below $1/3$ because
$C<C_*$ implies $\sqrt{1-C^2}>2/3$.
Also $\delta\le k/2$ and $1-s\le k^2/2$;
both follow from $s^2+c^2=1$ and $c= k s$.
For rising middle, $H=1$ and $T_R\le\delta/2$, so
\[
 D\le D_0+ k/4,\qquad e\le e_0+\tfrac34b( k)\le1.
\]
For descending middle, $T_R=0$ and
$ k d_\alpha= k\delta+h$.
Thus the height loss also lowers the outgoing wall, and
\[
 e\le\max\{3/4+5h/4,\ 1/2+h/2+(3/2)C_* k\}<1,
 \qquad 0\le h<1-s\le k^2/2.
\]
The last comparison follows at $ k= k_*$; both expressions
increase with $h$ and $ k$.
In both orientations $D>3/2$ and $e\ge0$.

Put $\mathcal H=(P-1/2)^2+(Q+1)^2$.
Expansion at the impulse gives
\[
 \mathcal H(\varepsilon+)-\mathcal H(0)
 =(1-e)(1-s)-Dc+2j[Ds-(1-e)c]+j^2.
\]
Since $Ds-(1-e)c>1$, this increases with $j\ge0$.
At $j=\delta$ it equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-impulse-energy}
 \delta^2[e-(1-e)s-Dc]<\delta^2.
\end{equation}
So the geometric impulse allowance is $\eta=\delta^2\le k^2/4$.
After the impulse, in the initial $P,Q>0$ sector,
$\mathcal H'=2(Q+1)(V-1/2)\le0$.
At the first $P$-zero, or at $\varepsilon$ if that zero occurred earlier,
\[
 Q+1\le\sqrt Z,\qquad Z=D^2-e(1-e)+\eta.
\]
The initial $Q$ component remains positive across the unused gap.
Every later positive $Q$ component has amplitude at most $1/8$:
it starts at $Q=0,P\le1$; while $P>0$, the shadow laws give
$P'\le-1$, $Q'\le P-1/2$, so its total positive increase is
at most $\int_0^{1/2}(1/2-r)\,\dd r=1/8$.
Once $P\le1/2$, $Q'$ is nonpositive, and once $P\le0$ it is
strictly negative. $P$ cannot rise while $Q>0$.
Thus only the initial component can have $U>1$, since that
requires $Q>1$ by \eqref{eq:partial-reflected-controls}.

After $P\le0$ and while $Q>1$, write $\rho$ for elapsed time.
Then $P'\le-1$, hence $P\le-\rho$ and, for $\rho\le1$,
$Q'\le-1/2-\rho/2$. For $-1\le P\le0$ use
$V\le(1-P)/2$; for $P<-1$ use $V\le-P$, giving $Q'\le-1$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-excess-decay}
 Q-1\le\sqrt Z-2-\rho/2-\rho^2/4,\qquad
 \ell=\sqrt{1+4(\sqrt Z-2)_+}-1.
\end{equation}
Once $0<Q\le1$, one has $P'\le-(1+Q)/2$ and $Q'\le-1/2$,
so this component cannot return to $Q>1$.
While $P,Q>0$, $Q'\ge-1$ and the impulse is upward;
therefore $P(r)\le e-Dr+r^2/2$.
The last possible excess time is at most
$\max(\varepsilon,D-\sqrt{D^2-2e})+\ell$.

Both orientations satisfy the single parameter budget
\eqref{eq:angular-parameter-budget}.
For rising middle only the span term is needed:
$\tfrac32(D-D_0)_+\le3 k/8$.
For descending middle $D\le D_0$ and the pressure bound gives
$(e-e_0-\tfrac34b( k))_+\le5 k^2/8$.
Its priced cost is at most $9 k^2/16\le3 k/8$.
If $Z\le4$ there is no excess after the zero or impulse.
Otherwise $D>2-2^{-6}$, so \cref{lem:angular-stopping-budget}
bounds the total time by $B( k)$ and gives $\ell<3/5$.
The other term of the maximum is covered too:
$\varepsilon=\arctan k< k$ and
$ k+3/5<B( k)$ for $0\le k\le k_*$.

The decay polynomial factors as
$(\ell-\rho)(1/2+(\ell+\rho)/4)$.
Its second factor is below $1/2+(3/5)/2=4/5$.
Since $U-1\le Q-1$ whenever $U>1$, the excess therefore satisfies
\[
 (U(r)-1)_+\le\tfrac45(B( k)-r)_+,\qquad
 (v(t)-1)_+\le\tfrac45(t-t_0)_+,\quad t_0=L-B( k).
\]
The prefix ends at $\theta=\arcsin(C-T_L)\le\arcsin C$.
The common cosine cut $\cos B_0>C_*$ gives
$\theta<L-B_0$, hence $(\theta-t_0)_+\le2 k/3$.
Its kernel is $\sin(\theta-t)/\cos\theta$, bounded by
$(\theta-t)/\cos\theta$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-duration-moment}
 \mathcal E_\theta
 \le\frac{4/5}{\cos\theta}\int_0^\theta(t-t_0)_+(\theta-t)\,\dd t
 =\frac{(4/5)(\theta-t_0)_+^3}{6\cos\theta}.
\end{equation}
Finally $\cos\theta>2/3$ and $c= k s\ge S_- k$.
By \eqref{eq:terminal-gap-payment},
\[
 (4/5)(2/3)^3k_*^2<\Gamma\,6(2/3)S_-.
\]
Thus the cubic overlap is less than $\Gamma c$ throughout
$0<k<k_*$. This is precisely the circle gap retained above.
\end{proof}
