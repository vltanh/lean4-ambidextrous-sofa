\section{Excluding a tilted full-turn maximum}\label{app:tilted-selector}

Throughout this appendix $U$ is a canonical full-turn maximum.
Reflect horizontally when necessary, and write
\[
 I=[-2C,2C],\quad J=[-C,C],\quad
 f_U(-C)=1-h,\quad f_U(C)=1,\quad h>0.
\]
The preliminary cuts and \cref{cor:full-three-tent-cut} give
$1/2<C<37/50$ and $0<h<1/3$.
Write $n=n_L(U)$, $n_\pm=n(\pm C)$ and $e_R=f_U(2C)$,
$e_L=f_U(-2C)$. The actual endpoint and wing identities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-endpoints}
 \begin{gathered}
 e_R=\tfrac12+\tfrac h4+(3n_+-n_-)/4,\quad
 e_L=\tfrac12-\tfrac{3h}4+(3n_--n_+)/4,\\
 E=e_R+e_L=1-h/2+(n_++n_-)/2,\quad
 \max_Jn\le E/2,\quad 2P_L(U)=L_{\rm w}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Let $F$ be the first support and $G$ the low-left-wing companion
support, with the uncharged middle facet removed, as in the tilted
support construction. Use $p,q,u,v$ for their velocities and regular
densities in \eqref{eq:variation-velocities}.

\subsection{A connected ordinary survivor}

The ordinary-sofa bound can be used once the niche stays below the
roof. We prove this by keeping every positive corner inside the
middle window. Each exterior wing then sees only one affine wall
family, so its niche is convex and cannot cross the concave roof.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tilted-box-confinement}
Every attached quadrant with positive apex height has its apex
abscissa in $(-C,C)$. The full niche is convex on each exterior
wing, and $n\le f_U$ on $I$. Consequently the survivor
$S=\{(x,y):x\in I,\ n(x)\le y\le f_U(x)\}$ is an ordinary sofa.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lift the low wing by $h$ and make the middle roof one, obtaining
$\widehat U$. Write $s=\sin t$, $c=\cos t$ and let
$\widehat F,\widehat G$ be its two supports.
For a horizontal-middle cap the box and top endpoints imply
\[
 \widehat F\le2Cc+s,\quad\widehat G\ge Cs+c,
 \quad\widehat z_x\le C(2-3s^2)-c+s.
\]
By \cref{lem:circle-corner-margin} with $h=0$,
$C(1-3s^2)<c-s$ throughout the preliminary width range.
Reflection gives $-C\le\widehat z_x\le C$.

Put $\delta=\arctan(h/(2C))$. For $t\ge\delta$ the actual
second support is on the low wing, and
$F=\widehat F$, $G=\widehat G-hc$.
Thus $z_x=\widehat z_x+hsc>-C$ in the open angular interval.
To prove its upper bound it suffices to show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-corner-margin}
 c-s-C(1-3s^2)-hsc>0.
\end{equation}
This is precisely \cref{lem:circle-corner-margin}: the unit circle's
quadratic chord bound handles $s^2\le1/3$, and its 3--4--5 tangent
handles $s^2\ge1/3$.
For $0<t<\delta$ the actual second support is $(C,1)$, and the
apex height is at most
$1-c-s+Csc=sc[C-2/(1+c+s)]<0$.
Such quadrants have no positive roof.

Every positive quadrant uses its descending first wall on the right
wing and its increasing second wall on the left wing.
The niche is therefore a maximum of affine functions and zero on
each wing, hence convex there; the box gives $n(\pm2C)=0$.
The endpoint box gives $n_\pm\le2/5$.
By \eqref{eq:tilted-endpoints},
$\max_J n\le7/10-h/4<1-h\le f_U$.
On a wing $f_U-n$ is concave and nonnegative at both endpoints,
so the same inequality holds throughout $I$.
Apply \cref{lem:ordinary-survivor}.
\end{proof}

In particular, with $G_0=2.2199$, convexity of the wing niche
and the stationary chord bounds give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-ordinary-bound}
 P_L(U)+C(3-E-3h/2)\le |S|\le G_0,\qquad
 P_L(U)\ge\sqrt{C^2+(1-h/2-E/2)^2}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the middle cap area is $2C-Ch$, while the exterior niche
is at most $C(n_++n_-)/2=C(E-1+h/2)$.
The two wing chords have vectors $(C,1-e_R)$ and
$(C,1-h-e_L)$; their average length is at least the length of
their average vector.

\subsection{The forward excess ends before endpoint tangency}

A positive niche maximum at the high middle endpoint must be tangent
at an angle $t\ge\arccos C$. We show that the first curvature
excess ends before this angle. The backward support formula then
charges the endpoint niche to the top overhang alone.

Put $J_*=(11/15)[(1/3)^2/4+(1/3)^3/6]$, the integrated envelope budget.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tilted-late-unit}
If $2/3<C<37/50$, then $u(t)\le1$ for almost every $t\ge11/15$.
Moreover, if the top face is $[C,C+T]\times\{1\}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-endpoint-leakage}
 \begin{gathered}
 n_+\le\frac{CT}{\sqrt{1-C^2}},\qquad
 J_F:=\int_0^L(u-1)_+\sin t\,\dd t\le J_*,\\
 \int_C^{2C}n\le(T+J_F)n_+/2.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $D=3C$, $B=e_R-1+h$ and $\gamma_0=\arccos(1-h)$.
The endpoint box $H(C)=1-\sqrt{1-C^2}<1/3$ and the sharper
low-wing box $n_-\le(H(C)-h)_+$ imply
\[
 -1/2+5h/4-(1/3-h)_+/4\le B\le-1/4+5h/4,
 \qquad -7/12<B<9/50.
\]
Only the initial positive-$q$ component can have $u>1$.
As in the reflected calculation in \cref{app:partial-reflection},
later positive components have $q\le1/8$.
Before its first $p$-zero $\beta$ the same-sign law gives $u=0$.
If $q(\beta)\le1$ no excess occurs. Otherwise put
$\eta=q(\beta)-1>0$.
On its only possible excess episode, at elapsed time $r\le1$,
\[
 p\le-r,\quad q'\le-(1+r)/2,\quad
 (u(\beta+r)-1)_+\le(\eta-r/2-r^2/4)_+.
\]
The duration is at most $\ell(\eta)=\sqrt{1+4\eta}-1$.
All bounds below give $\ell<1$, as required.

If $\beta\le\gamma_0$, the initial-floor solution in
\eqref{eq:initial-floor-solution} gives
$(q(\beta)+1)^2=D^2+B^2-1>4$.
Thus $B<0$, since $(111/50)^2+(9/50)^2<5$.
Also $D\sin\beta=1+B\cos\beta\le1$, so
$\beta<\pi/6$.
Finally $(q(\beta)+1)^2<(111/50)^2+(7/12)^2-1<(2+1/12)^2$,
so $\eta<1/12$, $\ell<1/6$, and the episode ends before
$(\pi+1)/6<11/15$.

If $\gamma_0<\beta$, then
$1+B(1-h)-D\sqrt{h(2-h)}>0$.
This forces $h<1/5$. The cutoff is $1-4/5$, from the same
3--4--5 circle direction used in the area comparison.
For $1/5\le h\le1/3$, the circle identity gives
\[
 h(2-h)-[(1+h)/2]^2=(1-h)(5h-1)/4\ge0.
\]
Thus $\sqrt{h(2-h)}\ge(1+h)/2$. Substituting this and
$B\le-1/4+5h/4$, $D\ge2$ makes the floor momentum nonpositive.
For $h<1/5$ the sharper pressure interval is
$-7/12+3h/2\le B\le-1/4+5h/4\le0$.
The energy used in \cref{lem:tilted-initial-energy} gives
\[
 (q(\beta)+1)^2\le
 Q(D,B,h)=D^2+B^2+B(1-h)-D\sqrt{h(2-h)}.
\]
The upper endpoint pressure at zero tilt is $3/4$. We compare with that trajectory.
For $0\le h\le1/5$, squaring gives $\sqrt{h(2-h)}\ge3h$.
The test $Q$ increases with $B$ on the displayed interval, and its
polynomial part at the upper endpoint is
\[
 (-1/4+5h/4)^2+(-1/4+5h/4)(1-h)
 =-3/16+7h/8+5h^2/16\le-3/16+h.
\]
Since $D\ge2$, the floor therefore removes at least $5h$ of energy:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-floor-loss}
 (2+\eta)^2+5h\le D^2-3/16.
\end{equation}
For this zero-tilt comparison, put
\[
 A_0=D-\sqrt{D^2-3/2},\qquad
 Y_0=\sqrt{D^2-3/16},\qquad Z_0=\sqrt{4Y_0-7}.
\]
The endpoint pressure bound gives
$0\le3/4+5h/4-D\beta+\beta^2/2$.
As $\sqrt{D^2-3/2}\ge3/2$, squaring shows
\[
 \beta\le D-\sqrt{D^2-3/2-5h/2}\le A_0+h.
\]
Thus tilt delays the first zero by at most $h$.
Its energy loss shortens the excess by at least the same amount.
Indeed $2+\eta\le Y_0<D<9/4$, so the sum of these two roots is below five.
By \eqref{eq:tilted-floor-loss},
\[
 Y_0-(2+\eta)
 =\frac{Y_0^2-(2+\eta)^2}{Y_0+(2+\eta)}\ge h.
\]
Similarly $1+\ell(\eta)\le Z_0<3/2$. Their sum is below four, giving
\[
 Z_0-[1+\ell(\eta)]
 =\frac{4[Y_0-(2+\eta)]}{Z_0+1+\ell(\eta)}\ge h.
\]
The delay and shortening cancel:
\[
 \beta+\ell(\eta)\le A_0+Z_0-1=W(D,3/4,0)<11/15
\]
by \cref{lem:joint-stopping-corner}. The same energy bound gives
$\eta<19/100$ and $\ell<1/3$ throughout the range.

The quadratic bound at the half-angle and the double-angle identity give
\[
 \cos(11/15)\ge2\left[1-\frac12\left(\frac{11}{30}\right)^2\right]^2-1
 >\frac{37}{50}.
\]

At a positive endpoint-active angle at $x=C$, confinement gives
strict companion slack, so stationarity has $B_x(t)=C$.
The outer first-support abscissa is at most $2C$, hence
$\cos t\le C$ and $t\ge\arccos C>11/15$.
The backward support formula then gives
\[
 a_t(C)=T\cot t+\frac1{\sin t}
       \int_t^L\sin(r-t)(u(r)-1)\,\dd r
       \le T\cot t\le\frac{CT}{\sqrt{1-C^2}}.
\]
Also $(F(t)-1)/\cos t\le C+T+J_F$.
Thus the convex right-wing niche vanishes by $C+T+J_F$ and has
area at most $(T+J_F)n_+/2$.
In every excess case above $\eta<19/100$ and $\ell<1/3$.
The quadratic envelope has integral less than $11/324$,
and occurs below $11/15$. Consequently
$J_F\le J_*=(11/15)(11/324)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The low endpoint is dry and the top overhang is positive}

The increasing companion-wall envelope has one zero. Its position
determines both the dry part of the middle interval and the available
source projection. Matching that projection to the exterior wings
identifies the top overhang. Comparing the same zero with walls from
the low middle point and the outer endpoint bounds the overhang.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tilted-dry-endpoint}
For $2/3<C<37/50$, the top overhang satisfies $T>0$ and
\[
 n_-=0,\qquad T\le\sqrt{h(2-h)},\qquad
 T\le\sqrt{1-e_L^2}-C,\qquad T<1-C.
\]
The late-unit and leakage conclusions of
\cref{lem:tilted-late-unit} hold as well.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $x_1=1-2C$ and consider the second-wall envelope
$\Psi(x)=\sup_{0<t<L}(G(t)-1+x\sin t)/\cos t$ for $x<x_1$.
It is finite, convex and nondecreasing. Its endpoint-angle limits
are $-h$ and $-\infty$. It is negative for $x\le-2C$ and
approaches a positive value at $x_1$, since the outer endpoint
$(-2C,e_L)$ has $e_L>0$.
It has a unique zero $x_0<x_1$: a zero maximum is attained at
an interior angle with strictly positive affine slope, which gives
strictly negative values to its left and positive values to its right.
For $x\le x_1$, the pinned top point $(C,1)$ gives
\[
 F(t)-1-x\cos t\ge(3C-1)\cos t+\sin t-1
             \ge\cos t+\sin t-1>0.
\]
For $x_1<x<C$, angles near $L$ have first wall
$(C+T-x)(L-t)+o(L-t)>0$ and second wall tending to $+\infty$.
The extra high-wing companion walls are nonpositive on $J$.
Hence $\{x\in(-C,C):n(x)>0\}=(\max(-C,x_0),C)$.

In this geometry the finite source projection identity has an exact
limit. On a compact interval to the left of $x_0$, all surrogate
second walls have a uniform negative gap. The high-point second
walls also have a negative gap outside arbitrarily small endpoint
angle neighborhoods. The coarse source bounds make their remaining
mass and horizontal projection $O(\delta)+o_i(1)$ for an angle
cutoff $\delta$. On compact subsets of the positive interval,
uniform barrier convergence gives the opposite inclusion.
Let the approximation index tend to infinity and then $\delta\to0$.
The finite projection identity and source balance yield
\[
 2C-T=C-\max(-C,x_0),\qquad T=(C+x_0)_+.
\]
This argument controls shallow finite graph pieces explicitly.

Suppose $T=0$. Reflect the local support equations as
$P=-q(L-r)$, $Q=-p(L-r)$, $U=v(L-r)$, $V=u(L-r)$.
Before the removed middle normal they have initial data
$(P(0),Q(0))=(e_L,3C-1)$ and the same arm and shadow laws.
Beyond that normal $U=0$ directly.
The box and endpoint equations give $3/20<e_L<3/4$.
Let $D=3C$ and $e=e_L$.
At the first $P$-zero, the energy calculation without an impulse
gives $(Q+1)^2\le D^2-e(1-e)$ and a crossing time at most
$D-\sqrt{D^2-2e}$.
The same quadratic excess envelope then applies.
The crossing and decay are bounded together by $W(D,e,0)$.
By \cref{lem:joint-stopping-corner}, the whole interval $0\le e\le3/4$
compares with $D=111/50$, $e=3/4$, where the sum is below $11/15$.
Also $e(1-e)\ge1/8$ on $[3/20,3/4]$, so the excess height
is below $1/5$ and its duration below $1/3$.
Later positive-$Q$ components have amplitude at most $1/8$.
Thus $v(t)\le1$ whenever $L-t\ge11/15$.

If $\Psi(-C)\ge0$, its maximum is attained at a proper angle
$t$ and has shifted companion abscissa $D_x(t)=-C$.
The outer supporting abscissa is $D_x-\sin t\ge-2C$,
so $\sin t\le C$ and $L-t\ge\arccos C>11/15$.
The entire prefix $[0,t]$ then has $v\le1$; its support formula
from $G(0)=1-h$, $G'(0)=C$ gives
\[
 \frac{G(t)-1-C\sin t}{\cos t}
 =1-h-\sec t+\frac1{\cos t}\int_0^t v(r)\sin(t-r)\,\dd r
 \le-h<0.
\]
This contradiction shows $\Psi(-C)<0$, hence $x_0>-C$,
contrary to $T=(C+x_0)_+=0$.
Therefore $T>0$, $x_0=-C+T$ and $n_-=0$.

The outer endpoint supplies a nonnegative second wall at
$x=-2C+\sqrt{1-e_L^2}$; the low middle point supplies one at
$x=-C+\sqrt{h(2-h)}$.
Comparing each with the unique zero $x_0$ gives the two square-root
bounds (if the test point is at least $x_1$, use $x_0<x_1$).
Finally $x_0<x_1$ implies $T<1-C$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The triangle payment above the radius width}

A dry low endpoint sharpens both endpoint heights. The resulting floor
payment decreases with tilt, so its zero-tilt value bounds every height.
This excludes widths above the boundary used by the initial energy test.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tilted-medium-width}
No canonical full-turn maximum with a dry low middle endpoint has
\(C_{\rm rad}\le C<37/50\) and \(0<h<1/3\), where
\(C_{\rm rad}\) is defined in \eqref{eq:tilted-radius-width}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(z=n_+\) and \(n_-=0\). Then
\(e_R=1/2+h/4+3z/4\) and \(e_L=1/2-3h/4-z/4\), with \(z<1/3\).
Use the lifted three-angle parameters of
\eqref{eq:three-tent-objective}. Put \(a=2C\), \(r=\sqrt2\),
\(k=r-1\), \(c_8=\cos(\pi/8)\), \(A=1-k\),
\(\gamma=1-r/2\), and \(t=2c_8-r\).
The outer deficits satisfy
\(w_R\le1/2-h/4\) and \(w_L<7/12+3h/4\).
The triangle and floor calculation gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
P_L(U)\le G_h(a,u,v)+L_{\rm a}(h),\\
L_{\rm a}(h)=\gamma[(D-h/4)^2+(D+1/12+3h/4)^2],\qquad D=1/2-t.
\end{gathered}
\]
A signed square bounds its positive-part square even if the endpoint
depth lies below the floor threshold. Thus this payment needs no
height subdivision.

The triangles fit throughout the stated range. Indeed the pressure sum
gives
\[
m-h/2\ge a-1/2-h/4
> a_{\rm rad}-1/2-(1/3)/4>3/4.
\]
Together with \(k>2/5\) and \(2\sin(\pi/8)>3/4\), this places both
inner crossings past the diagonal apex. Their outer zeros remain
inside \(J\), since \(C<4/5\). These are the same geometric
checks as in \cref{lem:full-three-tent-score}.

Use the shared supporting plane at the Newton comparison width
\(a_{\rm rad}\). Its clipping and width-derivative inequalities follow
from the Newton formulas in \eqref{eq:triangle-newton-parameters}
and \eqref{eq:tilted-radius-width}, throughout \(h\le1/3\).
Consequently
\[
G_h(a,u,v)\le B(a_{\rm rad})-kh/2-h^2/8
 -\tfrac29[s_0(a_{\rm rad})-Ah/4]^2.
\]
The dry-endpoint row of \cref{lem:triangle-tilt-payment} bounds the
right side plus \(L_{\rm a}\) at \(h=0\).
Its value is the dry-endpoint row of \cref{lem:triangle-newton-corners},
which is below \(\underline p\). This contradicts maximality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The last small-height alternative}

If either initial-floor energy test fails, the width must increase.
For a small tilt, the resulting gain in ordinary survivor area exceeds
the losses from the tilt and the short leaked niche. We compare those
quantities using chord lengths in a fixed unit direction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tilted-small-height}
No canonical full-turn maximum has $2/3<C<37/50$ and
$0<h\le h_0$, with $h_0$ defined by \eqref{eq:tilted-balance-height}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $P=P_L(U)$ and $\mathcal S=S$, the ordinary survivor of
\cref{lem:tilted-box-confinement}. By \cref{thm:ordinary-sofa-bound},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-2}
 |\mathcal S|\le G_0:=2.2199.
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Exact endpoint and leakage information}

Write the top face as \([C,C+T]\times\{1\}\), and put

\[
\begin{gathered}
z=n(C),\qquad s=\sqrt{h(2-h)},\quad
K=\frac{C}{\sqrt{1-C^2}},\\
J_F=\int_0^{\pi/2}(u(t)-1)_+\sin t\,\dd t.
\end{gathered}
\]

\Cref{lem:tilted-dry-endpoint} and the actual pressure equations give
\(T>0\) and \(n(-C)=0\). The triangle comparison in
\cref{lem:tilted-medium-width} also gives \(C<C_{\rm rad}\).

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-3}
e_R=\frac12+\frac h4+\frac{3z}{4},\qquad
e_L=\frac12-\frac{3h}{4}-\frac z4.
\end{equation}

The pinned low middle point and the endpoint estimate \eqref{eq:tilted-endpoint-leakage} imply

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-4}
T\le s,\qquad 0\le z\le KT,\qquad K\le K_+:=\mathcal N_5(2)/2.
\end{equation}

To see $T\le s$, use the signed second
wall envelope at its zero \(x_{\rm zero}=-C+T\). The low middle point
\((-C,1-h)\) gives the lower wall
\(1-h-\sec t+T\tan t\). Its value at \(\sin t=T\) is
\(1-h-\sqrt{1-T^2}\), and cannot be positive at an envelope zero.
Thus \(T^2\le h(2-h)\). We have \(T\le C<1\), so this angle is
proper. For the projection bound, the radius-width cut gives $9C^2<5$,
so $C<\mathcal N_5(2)/3$ and $\sqrt{1-C^2}>2/3$.
Dividing gives exactly $K<K_+$; the same radius supplies both bounds.

The early-end proof bounds all possible first-source excess by one
initial envelope

\[
(u(\alpha+\tau)-1)_+
\le\left(\varepsilon-\frac\tau2-\frac{\tau^2}{4}\right)_+,
\qquad \varepsilon<\frac{19}{100}.
\]

Take $d_0=1/3$. The parabola with initial height
$d_0/2+d_0^2/4>19/100$ vanishes at $d_0$, so it bounds both
the duration and the area of the excess:

\[
\int(u-1)_+\,dt
<\int_0^{d_0}\left(\frac{d_0}{2}+\frac{d_0^2}{4}
                    -\frac\tau2-\frac{\tau^2}{4}\right)d\tau
=\frac{d_0^2}{4}+\frac{d_0^3}{6}.
\]

All excess occurs before angle $11/15$, by the early-end estimate.
Using $\sin t\le t$ therefore gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-6}
J_F\le J_*:=\frac{11}{15}\left(\frac{d_0^2}{4}+\frac{d_0^3}{6}\right).
\end{equation}

\Cref{lem:tilted-late-unit} and convexity of the actual niche on the right wing give

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-7}
N_{\rm out}\le\frac{(T+J_F)z}{2}.
\end{equation}

The left wing contributes no outside niche: its niche roof is convex
with zero values at both endpoints. The middle cap area is \(2C-Ch\).
Thus

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-8}
|\mathcal S|\ge P+C(2-h)-\frac{(T+J_F)z}{2}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A 3--4--5 lower bound for the wing lengths}

The initial-energy test singles out the width
\[
 C_0=\frac{\sqrt{17}}6.
\]
The chord slope near this width is comparable to $1/(2C_0)$.
The unit direction of a $3$--$4$--$5$ triangle has a nearby slope
$3/4$ and gives an affine lower bound for both chord lengths. Its coefficients are exact:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-9}
 \lambda=\frac45,\qquad m=\frac3{20},\qquad a=\frac1{10}.
\end{equation}
Put $S_0=(14/5)C_0+3/10$.

The two charged-wing vertical drops are
\[
 r_R=1-e_R=\frac12-\frac h4-\frac{3z}{4},\qquad
 r_L=1-h-e_L=\frac12-\frac h4+\frac z4.
\]
The top overhang has length $T$, while the nonhorizontal right wing
has horizontal displacement $C-T$. The stationary length identity
and the two chord bounds yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-10}
 P\ge\frac12\left[T+
 \sqrt{(C-T)^2+r_R^2}+\sqrt{C^2+r_L^2}\right].
\end{equation}

Cauchy--Schwarz in the chosen unit direction gives
\[
 \sqrt{x^2+y^2}\ge\frac45x+\frac35y.
\]
Applying it to both chords gives
$P\ge4C/5+3/10-3(h+z)/20+T/10$.
Together with \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-8}, this becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-11}
 |\mathcal S|\ge S_0+\frac{14}{5}(C-C_0)
 -Ch-\frac3{20}(h+z)+\frac T{10}-\frac{(T+J_F)z}{2}.
\end{equation}

Use $C\le C_+:=\mathcal N_5(2)/3$, $T\ge z/K_+$, and $J_F\le J_*$.
Collecting the height and niche losses gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-12}
 D:=C_++\frac3{20},\qquad
 E:=\frac3{20}+\frac{J_*}{2}-\frac{1/10}{K_+}.
\end{equation}
Thus the common lower bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-13}
 |\mathcal S|\ge S_0+\frac{14}{5}(C-C_0)
 -Dh-Ez-\frac{Tz}{2}.
\end{equation}
One Newton step from $4$ gives an upper bound
$\mathcal N_{17}(4)$ for $\sqrt{17}$, and its reciprocal gives
the lower bound $17/\mathcal N_{17}(4)$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-14}
 S_0>\frac{14}{5}\frac{17}{6\mathcal N_{17}(4)}+\frac3{10}
     >G_0.
\end{equation}

Both tests use these same area prices. The balance-height bracket
$h_0\le h_+$ in \eqref{eq:tilted-balance-height} gives
\[
 \kappa=\frac1{2-h_+},\qquad
 r=\mathcal N_{h_+(2-h_+)}(1/3).
\]
The identity $s^2=h(2-h)$ gives $h\le\kappa s^2$ and $0<s<r$.
These bounds are generated from the energy balance, using its canonical interval
endpoint as the Newton seed. In particular $z\le K_+s$
and $h+z<2/3$.

\paragraph{The floor-energy test}

If the floor-energy test fails, completing its square forces an
increase in $C$. That width gain pays for the height and niche losses
in \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-13}.

Put

\[
d=3C,\qquad B=e_R-1+h=-\frac12+\frac{5h+3z}{4}.
\]

The second condition of \cref{lem:tilted-initial-energy} is

\[
Q:=d^2+B^2+B(1-h)-ds\le4.
\]

Suppose it fails. The exact square completion is

\[
Q=\left(3C-\frac s2\right)^2
+\frac9{16}(h+z)^2-\frac14,
\]

so

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-15}
\left(3C-\frac s2\right)^2
+\frac9{16}(h+z)^2>\frac{17}{4}.
\end{equation}

Write \(w=h+z\). The common bounds above give \(w<2/3\), and
\(3C-s/2>0\). Rationalizing the difference of the two positive
square roots gives

\[
\frac{\sqrt{17}}2-
\sqrt{\frac{17}{4}-\frac9{16}w^2}
=\frac{9w^2/16}{\sqrt{17}/2+
\sqrt{17/4-9w^2/16}}
<\frac9{64}w^2.
\]

The denominator exceeds four because \(w<2/3\). Therefore \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-15}
implies

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-16}
C-C_0>\frac s6-\frac3{64}(h+z)^2.
\end{equation}

Insert \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-16} into \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-13}
and use $z\le K_+s$ and $T\le s$. This gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-17}
 |\mathcal S|-S_0\ge
 As-Dh-\frac{K_+}{2}s^2-Q_0\left(h+K_+s\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where the coefficients come directly from the preceding bounds:
\[
 A=\frac{14/5}{6}-K_+E,\qquad
 Q_0=\frac{14}{5}\frac3{64}.
\]

The common height and sine bounds give
\[
h+K_+s\le\left(K_++\kappa r\right)s.
\]
All losses in \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-17} are therefore bounded by
one multiple of $s^2$. Put
\[
 L_r=D\kappa+\frac{K_+}{2}+
       Q_0\left(K_++\kappa r\right)^2.
\]
The area gain is at least $s(A-L_rs)$, whose bracket is smallest
at $s=r$. Substituting the defining formulas gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-18}
 A-L_rr>0.
\end{equation}
Thus $|\mathcal S|>S_0>G_0$, contradicting
\eqref{eq:tilted-sh-2}.

\paragraph{The radius failure gives a joint pressure budget}

Suppose the other energy test fails:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-19}
9C^2+B^2>5.
\end{equation}
The pressure has the useful form
\[
B=-\frac12+\frac54\left(h+\frac35z\right).
\]
The common bounds \(h\le h_0\), \(z\le K_+r\) put
\(-1/2<B<0\). Recall that
\(b_0=-1/2+5h_0/4\) and \(9C_{\rm rad}^2=5-b_0^2\).
Since \(C<C_{\rm rad}\), a failed radius test forces
\(B^2>b_0^2\), hence \(B<b_0\). The pressure equation therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-21}
\boxed{h+\frac35z\le h_0.}
\end{equation}
This is the available loss budget, with both terms retained together.
From the defining area prices,
\(E+r/2<(3/5)D\). Since \(T\le r\), the whole loss obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-22}
Dh+Ez+Tz/2\le D\left(h+\frac35z\right)\le Dh_0.
\end{equation}

Also \(|B|<1/2\), so \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-19} implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-20}
C>C_1:=\frac{\sqrt{5-1/4}}3=\frac{\sqrt{19}}6.
\end{equation}
This width gain pays for the pressure budget. Rationalizing the two
circle radii gives
\[
C_1-C_0=\frac{1}{3(\sqrt{19}+\sqrt{17})}>\frac1{27},
\]
because each root is less than \(9/2\). Finally
\((14/5)/27>Dh_0\), by the definitions of \(D,h_0\).
The shared area bound \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-13} now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-sh-23}
|\mathcal S|>S_0+\frac{14}{5}(C_1-C_0)-Dh_0>S_0>G_0.
\end{equation}
This contradicts \eqref{eq:tilted-sh-2}. No separate bound on \(z\)
or subdivision of the small-height interval is needed.
Both energy conditions in \cref{lem:tilted-initial-energy} therefore
hold. That lemma gives $u\le1$ on the whole first quarter, and
\cref{lem:tilted-first-unit-exclusion} excludes this tilted maximum.
\end{proof}
