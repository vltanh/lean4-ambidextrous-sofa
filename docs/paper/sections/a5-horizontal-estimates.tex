\section{The horizontal middle: width and curvature estimates}
\label{app:horizontal-estimates}

Throughout this appendix $L=\pi/2$. For a centered height-one cap
write $I=[-2C,2C]$, $J=[-C,C]$, $A=f_U$ and $n=n_L(U)$.
A horizontal canonical middle has $A=1$ on $J$, since the top
face meets $J$ by \cref{lem:selection-top}. We prove the following
input to the full-turn argument.

\begin{lemma}[Horizontal unit curvature]\label{lem:full-horizontal-unit}
Every canonical full maximum with horizontal middle satisfies
\[
 \frac12<C<C_H,
\]
where $C_H=\sqrt{17}/6$ is the endpoint-energy threshold when the
axis heights are one half.
Its top face is exactly $J\times\{1\}$, and its two regular
curvature densities satisfy $0\le u,v\le1$ almost everywhere.
There are unique $0<\beta<\gamma<L$ such that
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 p>0&(0,\beta),&p<0\quad(\beta,L),\\
 q>0&(0,\gamma),&q<0\quad(\gamma,L).
 \end{array}
\]
\end{lemma}

The preliminary estimates already give the range needed here.
The zero-tilt case of \cref{lem:preliminary-short} excludes $C\le1/2$,
and \cref{cor:full-three-tent-cut} excludes $C\ge37/50$.
Thus throughout the proof
\[
 \frac12<C<\frac{37}{50}.
\]
We will first bound how long curvature can exceed one. The key point
is that this excess ends before a corner can cut a middle endpoint.
The endpoint pressures then become exact, and the ordinary-sofa
bound supplies the width estimate.

\subsection{The top face and transfer between quarters}

The top face already contains the middle interval. Any extra top length
would consume part of the projection budget available to the positive
niche. The following argument shows that this budget is fully used.

For a full maximum the endpoint law gives, with $n_\pm=n(\pm C)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-pressure}
 \begin{gathered}
 e_R=\frac{2+3n_+-n_-}{4},\qquad
 e_L=\frac{2+3n_--n_+}{4},\\
 E=e_R+e_L=1+(n_++n_-)/2.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Both end heights are positive.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:horizontal-top}
If a horizontal canonical full maximum has $C\ge1/2$, its top
face is exactly $[-C,C]\times\{1\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the top face $[a,b]\times\{1\}$; it contains $J$.
As $t\downarrow0$, $F(t)\to2C$ and $G(t)=1-at+o(t)$.
For $a<x<2C-1$ the first wall tends to $+\infty$, and
the second is $(x-a)t+o(t)>0$. Thus $n(x)>0$ there.
Reflection gives positivity on $(-2C+1,b)$.
For $C\ge1/2$ these intervals overlap or touch and cover the
interior of $J$ except possibly one point.
The positive-barrier projection bound of
\cref{cor:variation-projection} therefore gives
\[
 2C\le2C-(b-a-2C).
\]
Since $b-a\ge2C$, equality holds and the endpoints are $-C,C$.
Only lower semicontinuity on compact positive-barrier intervals
is used in this projection bound.
\end{proof}

The endpoint support values now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-corner-ends}
 p(0)=e_R,\quad q(0)=3C-1,\quad
 p(L)=1-3C,\quad q(L)=-e_L.
\end{equation}
On $\{q>0\}$ the curvature and shadow laws imply $p'\le-1/2$;
on $\{p<0\}$ they imply $q'\le-1/2$.
Indeed, for $0<q\le1$ the bound $u\le(1+q)/2$ gives
$p'\le-(1+q)/2$; for $q>1$, $u\le q$ gives $p'\le-1$.
For $-1\le p<0$, $v\le(1-p)/2$ gives
$q'\le(p-1)/2$; for $p<-1$, $v\le-p$ gives $q'\le-1$.
The positive-positive sector also has
\[
 p'=-1-q\le-1,\qquad q'\le p-1/2.
\]
Consequently any positive-$q$ component beginning at $t_A>0$
has $q\le1/8$: until $p$ becomes negative,
$p(t_A+s)\le1-s$ and the increase of $q$ is at most
$\int_0^{1/2}(1/2-s)\,ds=1/8$; after that crossing $q$
decreases. At a crossing $p=0$ with $q>0$, $p'<0$,
so the crossing on such a component is unique.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint energy]\label{lem:horizontal-energy}
If $9C^2\le4+e_R(1-e_R)$, then $u\le1$ almost everywhere.
The reflected criterion with $e_L$ implies $v\le1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the initial positive-positive sector,
\[
 \mathcal E=(p-1/2)^2+(q+1)^2,\qquad
 \mathcal E'=2(q+1)(v-1/2)\le0.
\]
If $p$ crosses zero on the initial positive-$q$ component, at
that crossing
$(q+1)^2\le9C^2-e_R(1-e_R)\le4$.
Thereafter $q$ decreases on that component, and later positive
components have $q\le1/8$. If the initial component ends before
the crossing, its positive-positive part has $u=0$.
Thus whenever $q>1$, one has $u=0$; elsewhere $-1\le q\le1$
and $u\le\kappa(q)\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One controlled quarter controls both]
\label{lem:horizontal-transfer}
For a horizontal canonical full maximum with $C>1/2$, either
$u\le1$ or $v\le1$ almost everywhere implies both inequalities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two quarters share the same niche. If one curvature density stays
below one, its wall moves monotonically and makes part of that niche
visible. The source law on this visible part then constrains the other
density. We show that an excursion above one would force a growing
energy to finish below its starting value.

Reflect so that $v\le1$, and suppose $u>1$ on a set of positive
measure. Such a point has $q>1$ and belongs to the initial
positive-$q$ component after its $p=0$ crossing.
This component ends at $\tau<L$. Let $b_0$ be its unique
subsequent $q=1$ crossing; then $p<0<q<1$ on $(b_0,\tau)$.
Every future positive component has $q\le1/8$, so $u\le1$
on $(b_0,L)$.

For the inner corner $c=(F-1)u(t)+(G-1)u^\perp(t)$ put
\[
 B=c+p u^\perp(t),\qquad D=c-q u(t).
\]
Differentiation gives
$B'=(u-1)u^\perp(t)$ and $D'=(1-v)u(t)$.
The top endpoints give $D(0)=(-C,0)$ and $B(L)=(C,0)$.
Thus $D_x,D_y$ are globally nondecreasing, and $B_x$ is
nondecreasing after $b_0$. On $(b_0,\tau)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-visible-window}
 -C\le D_x<c_x<B_x\le C,\qquad c_y=D_y+q\sin t>0.
\end{equation}
The $D$ graph has positive height wherever its arc density $1-v$
is nonzero: the derivative of $D_y$ vanishes almost everywhere
on its zero level set. Its contributing points lie strictly inside $J$.

The wall parameter derivatives are
\[
 \partial_t a_t(x)=\frac{x-B_x(t)}{\sin^2t},\qquad
 \partial_t b_t(x)=\frac{x-D_x(t)}{\cos^2t}.
\]
Global monotonicity of $D_x$ makes $D(t)$ a maximum of the
entire second-wall family, with positive companion gap $q/\sin t$.
Unless $D$ is constant on a parameter interval, which has zero
arc contribution, its active source parameter is unique.
At $c(t)$ every earlier second wall is strictly lower, because
$c_x(t)-D_x(s)\ge q(t)\cos t>0$ for $s<t$;
every later first wall is strictly lower, because
$c_x(t)-B_x(s)\le p(t)\sin t<0$ for $s>t$.
The corner likewise has unique global source parameter and
strictly decreasing abscissa.
For $K=[t_0,t_1]\subset(b_0,\tau)$ the two graph images are
disjoint, since $D_x(t_1)<c_x(t_1)$.

We can therefore add their finite source fluxes. On positive-height
compact subarcs, active finite source parameters converge to the
unique limiting parameter: any contrary subsequence would supply
a different maximizing parameter by uniform wall convergence.
The angles stay away from the axes, so graph slopes are bounded.
With increasing abscissa the finite vector flux is
\[
 (\dd x,\dd y)=-u^\perp(t)\,\dd\nu_i^f
                         +u(t)\,\dd\nu_i^g.
\]
Its first component is Lebesgue measure, and its second converges
by uniform roof convergence and the common slope bound.
Solving the two-direction system gives
$\dd\nu^g=\cos t\,\dd x+\sin t\,\dd y$.
The $D$ graph therefore contributes $(1-v)\dd t$ to this source,
and the reverse-oriented corner contributes $-p\dd t$.
Exhaustion removes constant and zero-height pieces.
Source equality from \cref{lem:variation-sources} yields
\[
 2v\ge1-p\quad\hbox{almost everywhere on }(b_0,\tau).
\]
Since $v\le1$, $p\ge-1$ there. The upper curvature bound gives
the reverse inequality $v\le(1-p)/2$, hence equality.
Set $x=1-p$, $y=1+q$. Then
\[
 x'=1+q-u\ge y/2,\qquad y'=-x/2,\qquad
 (x^2+y^2)'\ge0.
\]
At $b_0$, $y=2$ and $x>1$, so this energy exceeds five;
at $\tau$, $y=1$ and $x\le2$, so it is at most five.
This contradiction proves the transfer.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Curvature excess ends before endpoint contact}

The box bounds the endpoint niches by
\[
 0\le n_\pm\le H(C):=1-\sqrt{1-C^2}.
\]
Indeed, $a_t(C)\le1+(C\cos t-1)/\sin t$, whose maximum
is $H(C)$; reflection gives the other endpoint.
By \eqref{eq:horizontal-pressure}, either axis height $e$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-box-pressure}
 \frac12-\frac{H(C)}4\le e\le\frac12+\frac{3H(C)}4.
\end{equation}
If $C\le2/3$, then $9C^2\le4\le4+e(1-e)$ because
$0\le e\le1$. The endpoint-energy lemma already gives both unit
bounds in this range. We therefore consider $C>2/3$.

\begin{lemma}[Dry middle endpoints]\label{lem:horizontal-dry}
If $2/3<C<37/50$, then $n(-C)=n(C)=0$ and $e_R=e_L=1/2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the right endpoint $e=e_R$. As in the endpoint-energy proof,
any $u>1$ must occur on the initial positive-$q$ component, after
its unique $p=0$ crossing $t_A$. Before that crossing,
$q'\ge-1$ and $p'=-1-q$, so
\[
 p(t)\le e-3Ct+t^2/2.
\]
The circle identity gives a useful uniform bound on the pressure.
For $C\le4/5$, $\sqrt{1-C^2}\ge3/5$, hence
\[
 H(C)=\frac{C^2}{1+\sqrt{1-C^2}}\le\frac58C^2,
 \qquad e\le\frac12+\frac{15}{32}C^2.
\]
The concave quadratic
\[
 \frac65C-\frac2{25}-\frac12-\frac{15}{32}C^2
\]
is positive at both endpoints of $[2/3,4/5]$, so it is positive
throughout. Thus $p$ must cross zero by $t=2/5$, unless the
positive-$q$ component has already ended; that case has no excess.

We next bound the duration after the crossing. Since $C<37/50$,
$H(C)<1/3$ and \eqref{eq:horizontal-box-pressure} gives
$1/4\le e\le3/4$. The initial energy therefore satisfies
\[
 9C^2-e(1-e)
 \le9(37/50)^2-3/16
 <\left(2+\frac{(1/3)^2+2/3}{4}\right)^2.
\]
At the crossing, this implies $q(t_A)-1\le7/36$.
While $q>1$, $p'\le-1$ and
\[
 p(t_A+\tau)\le-\tau,\qquad
 q'\le-(1+\tau)/2\quad(0\le\tau\le1).
\]
For $p\ge-1$ the second bound follows from $v\le(1-p)/2$;
for $p<-1$ the stronger bound $q'\le-1$ suffices.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-leakage-envelope}
 (u(t_A+\tau)-1)_+
 \le\left(\frac7{36}-\frac\tau2-\frac{\tau^2}4\right)_+.
\end{equation}
The right side vanishes at $\tau=1/3$. Later positive-$q$
components have $q\le1/8$, as proved above. Thus every possible
curvature excess has ended by
\[
 \frac25+\frac13=\frac{11}{15}.
\]
These are coarse crossing and duration bounds for the whole width
range. We need only that their sum precedes endpoint contact.
The quadratic bound at $t/2$ gives
$\cos t=2\cos^2(t/2)-1\ge2(1-t^2/8)^2-1$ at $t=11/15$.
This exceeds $37/50$, hence $11/15<\arccos C$.

The exact top endpoint data $F(L)=1$, $F'(L)=-C$ give
\[
 a_t(C)=\frac1{\sin t}\int_t^L\sin(s-t)(u(s)-1)\,\dd s.
\]
For $t\ge\arccos C$ the integrand is nonpositive, so $a_t(C)\le0$.
For earlier angles, the wall increases: if its supporting point
has abscissa $x_t\le2C$, then
\[
 \partial_t a_t(C)
 =\frac{C-x_t+\cos t}{\sin^2t}\ge0
 \qquad(t\le\arccos C).
\]
It is therefore bounded above by the nonpositive wall at
$\arccos C$. Every visited angle has $a_t(C)\le0$, proving
$n(C)=0$. Reflection gives $n(-C)=0$, and the endpoint-pressure
identities give $e_R=e_L=1/2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Circle confinement and the ordinary survivor}

The next circle inequality keeps the corners inside the middle
window. We state it with a tilt parameter because the same argument
will be used in \cref{app:tilted-selector}.

\begin{lemma}[Circle margin]\label{lem:circle-corner-margin}
If $s,c>0$, $s^2+c^2=1$, $1/2\le C\le37/50$, and $h\le1/3$, then
\[
 c-s-C(1-3s^2)-hsc>0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $D=37/50$, $H=1/3$. When $s^2\le1/3$, $c\ge4/5$,
so the circle identity gives
\[
 c=1-\frac{s^2}{1+c}\ge1-\frac59s^2.
\]
Also the tangent to $s^3$ at $s=1/3$ lies below that cubic:
\[
 s^3-\frac s3+\frac2{27}
 =(s-1/3)^2(s+2/3)\ge0.
\]
Substituting these two bounds into the margin gives the quadratic
$a s^2+b s+d$, where
\[
 a=3D-5/9,\quad b=-(1+H)+5H/27,\quad d=1-D-10H/243.
\]
Here $a>0$ and $4ad-b^2>0$. Thus completing the square proves
positivity for every $s$.

When $s^2\ge1/3$, decrease $C$ to $1/2$ and increase $h$ to $2/5$.
The resulting margin factors as
\[
 c\left[1-\frac25s-\frac{c(3s+1)}{2(1+s)}\right].
\]
The 3--4--5 tangent inequality $3s+4c\le5$ bounds the bracket below by
\[
 \frac{15-36s+29s^2}{40(1+s)}.
\]
Its numerator is positive because $36^2<4\cdot29\cdot15$.
\end{proof}

Continue to assume $C>2/3$. For the horizontal cap, put $s=\sin t$, $c=\cos t$.
The box and left top endpoint imply
$F\le2Cc+s$ and $G\ge Cs+c$, whence
\[
 c_x\le C(2-3s^2)-c+s<C
\]
by \cref{lem:circle-corner-margin} with $h=0$.
Reflection gives $-C<c_x$. Each exterior wing therefore sees only
one affine wall from every positive quadrant. Its niche is convex.
The box gives $n(\pm2C)=0$, and \cref{lem:horizontal-dry} gives
$n(\pm C)=0$. Convexity and nonnegativity make both wings dry.
On $J$, \cref{cor:variation-green} gives $n\le E/2=1/2<A$.
Thus $n\le A$ on $I$, and \cref{lem:ordinary-survivor} makes the
actual survivor an ordinary sofa.

The wing-length identity and the chord bound, now with both axis
heights equal to one half, give
\[
 P_L(U)=L_{\rm wing}/2\ge\sqrt{C^2+1/4}.
\]
The survivor has area $P_L(U)+2C$, since the wings are dry.
The ordinary-sofa bound therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-ordinary-necessary}
 2C+\sqrt{C^2+1/4}\le G_0:=2.2199.
\end{equation}
The 3--4--5 supporting direction bounds the square root below by
$4C/5+3/10$. At $C=C_H=\sqrt{17}/6$ this yields
\[
 2C_H+\sqrt{C_H^2+1/4}
 \ge\frac{14}{5}\frac{\sqrt{17}}6+\frac3{10}>G_0.
\]
The last comparison follows by squaring
$\sqrt{17}>(G_0-3/10)\,15/7$; all terms are positive.
The left side of \eqref{eq:horizontal-ordinary-necessary} increases
with $C$, so $C<C_H$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:full-horizontal-unit}]
The preliminary range gives $1/2<C<37/50$ and
\cref{lem:horizontal-top} gives the exact top face.
If $C\le2/3$, both endpoint-energy criteria hold directly.
If $C>2/3$, the preceding argument gives dry endpoints,
$e_R=e_L=1/2$, and $C<C_H=\sqrt{17}/6$. Hence
\[
 9C^2<17/4=4+e_R(1-e_R)=4+e_L(1-e_L),
\]
so \cref{lem:horizontal-energy,lem:horizontal-transfer} give $u,v\le1$.
In either case $C<C_H$; use $2/3<C_H$ in the first case.

For the final sign statement, $B_x,D_x$ are nondecreasing and
\[
 B_x-D_x=q\cos t-p\sin t\ge4C-2>0,
\]
because $B_x\ge B_x(0)=2C-1$ and
$D_x\le D_x(L)=1-2C$.
At any zero of $p$, this strict inequality gives $q>0$,
so $p'=u-1-q<0$ nearby; a return through zero is impossible.
At any zero of $q$, it gives $p<0$, so $q'=v-1+p<0$
nearby, likewise excluding a return.
The endpoint signs in \eqref{eq:horizontal-corner-ends}
give one crossing each. They cannot coincide, and at the $p$
crossing $q>0$, so their order is $\beta<\gamma$.
\end{proof}
